# T2543 - Composition step 4c-i: the caption and tab bar compose as an overlay visual above the desk on an opaque elevated window - a persistent mirror of the caption band, painted by the same GDI painters into a memory DC, uploaded by dirty rect

## Summary

T2510 split, part i. paintChromeRaw's paintCaption/paintTabBar draw into a persistent caption-band mirror DIB (full client width x chromeHeight) instead of the window DC whenever chrome_host carries the desk; the mirror is uploaded to a premultiplied caption visual added above the desk visual (one commit per paint, BeginPaint's rect as the dirty rect). WM_PRINTCLIENT still paints the caller's DC. Oracle: WGC ghost grab knob on vs off matches on the caption and tab strip (extend scripts\comp-desk-onscreen.ps1 or a sibling), hover and press states included; -NegativeControl goes red.

## In plain terms

The strip at the top of the window - the title, the tabs, and the minimise /
maximise / close buttons - was still hand-drawn into the window each time,
even with the compositor switch on (`GHOZTTY_COMP_CHROME=1`), while the
coloured desk below it already went to the system compositor. Now the top
strip goes to the compositor too, in the same frame as the desk, and only the
part that changed (a button lighting up, say) is sent. It looks exactly the
same. This is one of the pieces the smooth window-edge resize is waiting on.
The switch is still off by default.

## Goals

- [x] With the switch on and the desk on the compositor, the title strip and tabs are a compositor layer above the desk, and the window stops drawing them by hand
- [x] Only the changed part goes up, and the strip and the desk change in the same frame
- [x] Where the desk falls back to hand-drawing (a hero tab, rearrange headers, a see-through window), the strip falls back with it, whole, with nothing left stale
- [x] On screen, the composed strip matches the hand-drawn one, pressed buttons included

## Design (2026-10-31)

`chrome_comp.Mirror` is a persistent top-down DIB, client width x
`chromeHeight()`, in a memory DC whose origin is the client's. With a chrome
host that can carry the desk (opaque, not under `WM_PRINTCLIENT`, not the test
fill) `paintChromeRaw` hands that DC to `paintCaption` and `paintTabBar`
unchanged - both already double-buffer and BitBlt their whole band to
whatever DC they are given, so the mirror is complete after every paint. Once
the paint knows where the desk went (`captionBandFinish`, after the dividers):

- desk on the visual -> `Host.capUpload` sends the paint's clip box
  (`bandDirty`) into the caption surface; a new band, size or host sends the
  whole mirror;
- desk on GDI -> the mirror's clip-box part is blitted into the window, as
  before; on the paint where the band just came OFF the compositor the whole
  mirror goes through an unclipped `GetDCEx` (the window's band was never
  painted while it was up).

The target's root is now a container visual: the desk is its bottom child and
the caption is added with `insertAbove=TRUE` naming the desk, so neither
relies on what a null reference means. The caption lives only while the desk
does (`deskHide` takes both). A paint batches its uploads (`beginBatch` /
`flush`): the desk and the band reach the screen in ONE commit, which is the
property the resize work needs. Busy-light uploads outside a paint still
commit on their own.

Choices made here (mechanism, not experience): the caption surface is
`DXGI_ALPHA_MODE_IGNORE`, not premultiplied as the summary said - GDI leaves
the alpha byte at 0 and every band pixel is painted on an opaque window, so
ignore-alpha is the same picture without a per-pixel fix-up. A failing
caption step logs `chrome caption visual failed at <stage>` and parks the band
on GDI over the desk visual (the pre-T2543 arrangement) until the host is
rebuilt. Debug seam for the oracle's negative control:
`GHOZTTY_COMP_CHROME_CAPTION_FREEZE=1` drops every dirty-rect band upload.
