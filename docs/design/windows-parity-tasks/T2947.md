# T2947 - A glass pane's backdrop slice is re-cropped from the full 4K desk on every glide tick it moves (~0.33 ms for a 2000+ px pane, memory-bound reading every 4th desk row); crop from a quarter-resolution copy of the desk instead

## Summary

Found by T2946. On a 4K fold glide, `syncGlassBackdrops` hands every moved
terminal a new quarter-resolution slice of the desk each tick
(`Surface.setPaneBackdrop` -> `desk_palette.cropDeskRegionRgba`). After
T2946 (recycled slice buffers, whole-texel stores) the slice still costs
~70 us for a 281 px pane and ~325-370 us for a 2000-2600 px one, ~0.35 ms
median of a heavy tick (p90 0.6 ms, max 1.5 ms). The crop is nearest-neighbour
at 1/4 scale, so each slice row reads a whole 4K desk row span for one texel
in four: it is bound by reading the 32 MB desk, not by the writes.

Fix: keep a quarter-resolution copy of the cached backdrop (built once per
desk, i.e. per window size / variant, never per tick) and crop each slice
from it - a quarter of the rows and a quarter of the columns to read. The
desk is a smooth gradient, so a sample a pixel or two off its current phase
is invisible; check the T2712 seam test still holds and add a unit test that
the quarter crop matches the full crop within 1 level per channel on a
gradient desk.

Measure with T2946's runner (`temp/t2946-ab.ps1`, ring trace; not committed - its shape is in T2946's progress log) - the
`stack trace vacated tail` line's `glass - under` is this step.

## In plain terms

With see-through ("glass") panes, every pane that moves during a fold
animation gets a fresh picture of the background behind it, every frame. On a
4K screen making those pictures cost about half a millisecond per frame,
because each one was cut out of the full-size background. Now they are cut
from a background kept at a quarter of the size and copied row by row. That
part of the frame costs about a third of what it did, so the 4K animation has
a little more room before it drops a frame.
