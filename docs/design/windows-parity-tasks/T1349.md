# T1349 - The banner rebuilds its whole glass card on every animation frame

## Summary

Found while fixing T1344. BannerOverlay.paintCardBackdrop treats the cached card surface as stale whenever the band width or height changed, and a collapse/expand changes the height on every one of ~11 frames - so card.render (rounded rect, elevation shadow, wash, at pane width) runs from scratch per frame, and a DIB section is created and destroyed with it. It is CPU work in the middle of an animation, which is the shape that drops frames and reads as stutter. Options worth measuring: render the card at the SETTLED height once and blit a sub-rect per frame, or keep the surface at the largest height seen and reuse it. Measure the per-frame cost first (the paint already logs a frame line).

## In plain terms

Collapsing or expanding a banner redraws its glass card from scratch about
eleven times in a fifth of a second. On a wide, high-resolution pane that
redraw used most of each frame's time budget, which is how an animation
stutters. The card now draws about three times faster and looks exactly the
same, pixel for pixel, and it no longer throws away and re-creates its drawing
surface on every frame.

## Goals

- [x] Measure the per-frame cost before changing anything.
- [x] Make the card render cheaper without changing a single pixel of it.
- [x] Stop creating and destroying the backdrop's DIB section on every frame.
- [x] Cover both with tests that go red if either regresses.

| 900 px @1.0 | 0.95 ms | 0.34 ms | 5.86 ms | 2.30 ms |
| 1800 px @1.5 | 2.93 ms | 0.99 ms | 17.52 ms | 6.47 ms |
| 3000 px @2.0 | 6.47 ms | 2.23 ms | 38.79 ms | 14.16 ms |

Bench: a scratch `zig test -O <mode>` that renders the card at 12 heights
from 60 to 260 dip, the shape of one toggle.

## Decision: faster exact render, not a cached slice

The Summary offered two cache shapes (render the settled height once and
blit a sub-rect; keep the largest surface and reuse it). Both change what
the frames LOOK like: the card's sheen and bottom shade are fractions of its
height (Mac's `EllipticalGradient`/`LinearGradient` on `GlassCard`), so on Mac
they re-scale every frame, and a cropped or nine-sliced cached card would
show a stretched or truncated highlight mid-animation. So the card is still
rendered at every frame's own height. What changed is how much work that is:

- **Mirror** - the card, its shadow and both specular ellipses are centred,
  so the right half reuses the left half's cover (and, where nothing
  underneath can matter, the finished pixel). Taken only when the centre is
  exactly `w/2` in floats; otherwise both halves are computed as before.
- **Row-constant interior** - a row the sheen cannot reach (with margin)
  has one interior colour in the opaque modes.
- **Straight edge runs** - on a top/bottom edge row the SDF cover is the same
  at every column between the corners (`qx <= min(0, qy)`), so it is computed
  once per row.
- **Vector interior** - the sheen-lit interior is done eight pixels at a time
  with the same per-lane arithmetic.

`renderReference` (the old walk, verbatim) stays in the tests as the oracle.

Separately, `BannerOverlay.buildCardSurface` now reuses the DIB section
whenever the width matches and it is tall enough; a new one is sized to the
whole client, which is the tallest band an animation can ask for.

      its size` now asserts the section count does not move across the
      animation and that it is sized to the client. Negative control (reuse
      forced off): red, `expected 2, found 13`.
- [x] Per-frame cost about 3x lower (table above).
