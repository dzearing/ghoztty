# T2873 - A fold-glide tick that resizes a pane waits for that pane's new-size frame, and 3 of ~19 such waits a run cost a whole 120 Hz vblank (6.7-8.6 ms), so the tick drops a frame

## Summary

Split from T2732 (2026-11-09). New tick-trace fields (step/end/live/flush) show that 4 of the 10 over-budget ticks in two release runs at 120 Hz are the layout pass's anti-blink frame wait (endFrameWaitBatch, T1343/T1476): waits=1, timeouts=0, wait_us 6736-8591, place+paint only ~2 ms. ~19 waits a run land every ~12 ticks; most cost 0.3-1.6 ms. The long ones look like a renderer whose present is held a vblank (T2788's residual: the previous frame still in flight). Find which pane resizes on those ticks and why its frame takes a vblank; do NOT trade the T1476 blink for the frame - the wait's guarantee stays. Probe: glide-probe.ps1 in session 7e867439's scratchpad, T2455_TRACE=1, ticks2.py.

## In plain terms

While you drag a divider back and a folded pane opens out of its strip, the
app asks that pane for a snapshot of itself (the picture the opening card
shows until the live pane takes over). Taking that snapshot made the pane
show its new frame several milliseconds late, and the whole window waited for
it, so those frames of the glide stuttered. The snapshot is now taken just
after the pane shows its frame, so nothing waits on it.

## Root cause

Measured with a temporary per-pane present stamp on the long waits plus the
resize-trace renderer stages (release build, glide probe, 5 runs): every long
wait was on the ONE pane opening out of the fold strip (`stack unfold carries
its frame`), and its `Present` itself took 27-84 us - no vblank hold. The 4-10
ms sat between `frame_ctx.complete` and `drawFrameEnd`, which is exactly the
`heroSnapshot` defer: `askOpeningFrame` (T2458) asks the opening card's pane
for an exact-size readback once per size, and the capture - a synchronous
`glReadPixels` behind the comp path's interop blit ("Pixel transfer is
synchronized with 3D rendering") - ran BEFORE the swap, so the layout pass's
frame wait sat behind it.

## Fix

`generic.zig`: the `heroSnapshot` defer is registered before the
`drawFrameEnd` defer, so it runs after the swap and after `signalFrameDrawn`.
It reads the renderer's own target (`last_target`), never the back buffer, so
the bytes are the same frame. Side effect: the readback is cheaper there too
(1.1-1.6 ms typical vs 4-8 ms before the swap). The T1476 wait guarantee is
untouched - the wait still ends only on a present at the new size; it just
no longer waits on the snapshot.
