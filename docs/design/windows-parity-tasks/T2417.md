# T2417 - Port Mac 7e2d0a9da's glide rules to the Windows stack (T2415 split)

## In plain terms

When the panes glide (folding into blades, or the sidebar opening and
closing), the blades already folded at the edge should move smoothly with
everything else, or stay still. On Windows they jump to their new spot at
once while the panes around them are still gliding. Mac fixed the same
thing today.

## Summary

Mac reference: **7e2d0a9da** (2026-10-28, `PaneStage.swift`):
"the grid never steps toward a layout it has not been laid out at, so blades
hold still; strips tween rect to rect". Controller directive on T2415: port
that rule.

What the Windows code does today (read 2026-10-28, `pane_stack_slide.plan`):

- **A strip that stays a strip never glides.** `plan` sends every
  tucked-before-and-after card to `holdReach` and `continue`s, so it has no
  `Card` in the slide. `holdReach` covers only the T2312 case, where the reach
  grows and the outer edge stays put. In every other case the strip moves at
  once to its new rect. That includes the sidebar column shift
  (`planStackSlide`'s `shifted` branch), where every on-show card glides from
  where it was drawn. So the blades jump the whole shift in one frame while
  the cards beside them glide.
- **Mac's across-stack rule.** Mac's held strips now ease across the stack
  with the glide instead of taking the target's size in the first frame. On
  Windows, `Slide.shownStrip` returns `heldStrip(tab, ...)`, built from the
  laid-out `tab`. Its across-stack extent and position are the target's at
  once.

Port:

1. A stays-tucked strip whose strip rect changed (moved, or resized other
   than a pure T2312 reach gain) gets a glide entry from its last strip rect
   to its new one, with all four edges following. It must be **strip-only**:
   the tucked pane stays hidden (`placeGrid`'s `stackSlideRect` path must not
   show it), and only the parent-painted strip (`shownStripTab`,
   `paintStackStrips`, the flat gutter exclusion) reads the drawn rect. Keep
   T2312's outer-edge hold. When both happen, apply the held length to the
   eased rect by its outer edge, the way Mac's `stepGlide` does.
2. The laid-out gate: Mac takes no eased step (`k = 0`) while the stage's
   frame does not yet have the size the targets were computed for, or while
   the column shift's move is still on its way (`awaitingMove`, at most
   `moveWait` = 150 ms). Windows lays out synchronously (`planStackSlide`
   runs inside the same `layoutSplits` pass that sets the grid rect), so
   first check whether this case can happen here at all. The candidate is the
   sidebar collapse/expand: if the column animates over several frames, does
   the grid rect move per frame and end the slide (`!same_view` without a
   column shift calls `endStackSlide`, so the cards snap)? Port the gate only
   if it can; otherwise record why it cannot.

Tests: unit tests in `pane_stack_slide.zig` (a column-shift plan carries every
stays-tucked strip from its old rect to its new one; a pure reach gain still
holds by its outer edge), plus an acceptance row in `stack-fold-slide.ps1` or
`pane-sidebar-*`: sidebar collapse with a folded stack, where the strip's
drawn rect passes through 2+ intermediate positions (an oracle log line).

## Validation criteria

- [ ] Stays-tucked strips glide rect to rect through a sidebar column shift; the pane behind each stays hidden.
- [ ] T2312's outer-edge hold is unchanged (existing tests green).
- [ ] The laid-out gate is ported, or shown not to apply on Windows, with the reason recorded.
