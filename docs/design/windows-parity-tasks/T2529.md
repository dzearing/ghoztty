# T2529 - A window-edge resize step still recreates the desk's comp bitmap (and out, when translucent) at the new size and composes it whole: rest_us 2.5 ms median per step at ReleaseSafe, the largest desk cost left after T2520

## Summary

Measured by T2520 (2026-10-30, resize-sync-onscreen.ps1 -Exe zig-out-rsafe, GHOZTTY_RESIZE_TRACE desk trace): after T2520 the desk build is ~0.56 ms but rest_us (compose + lights + names + blit after ensureComp) is 1.5-3.1 ms, median 2.5 ms, and ensureComp destroys and creates a w x h DIB section every step, so the whole compose writes into freshly faulted pages (the same first-touch cost T2520 found was most of the desk). Options: keep comp as ONE DIB section at the gesture's largest size and BitBlt the w x h corner (compose already takes w/h; it would need a stride), or a CreateDIBSection over a reused file-mapping section so the pages stay resident. Validate with the same trace: rest_us down by the fault share, a pixel test that a settled window's paint is unchanged, pane-elevation.ps1 green.

## In plain terms

When you drag a window's edge, every step of the drag rebuilt the picture
behind the panes in brand-new memory, and Windows had to hand that memory over
page by page as it was first written. This makes the drag reuse the same
memory each step. It turned out to be a small part of the cost: the drag gets
about 0.2 ms faster per step, and the bigger cost, painting the cards
themselves, now has its own task (T2537).

## Goals

- [x] the comp and translucent out bitmaps reuse a kept memory section across sizes
- [x] no page faults left in the compose or blit of a resize step
- [x] the remaining cost measured part by part, and the largest part filed

## Findings

A/B in one ReleaseSafe build (a temporary knob, since removed), 127 resize
steps each, resize-sync-onscreen.ps1, GHOZTTY_RESIZE_TRACE:

| | rest_us | compose_us | blit_us | faults compose / blit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| fresh DIB per step (before) | 2221 | 1988 | 217 | 936 / 0 |
| kept section, blit through the DC | 2410 | 1685 | 728 | 5 / 852 |
| kept section, blit from the kept view (this change) | 2023 | 1773 | 237 | 5 / 0 |

- The task's premise was only partly true. The faults were real (~940 per step,
  i.e. the whole 4 MB), but they cost ~0.2-0.4 ms, not most of the 2.5 ms.
  The compose is compute: the desk memcpy and every card's interior, shadow and
  rim painted over the whole window. Filed as **T2537** (P0, M1, now a dep of
  T2516).
- A DIB created over a section is still a NEW view per bitmap, so the faults
  just move to whoever reads first. With the CPU writing through our kept view
  they moved into GDI's BitBlt (852 soft faults, blit 0.73 ms). The fix reads
  the opaque blit from the kept view with StretchDIBits (`blitPixels`, the same
  shape HeroCarousel.blitDesk already uses). The translucent `out` blit stays on
  its DC, because a DIB blit from memory does not promise to keep the alpha
  byte the translucent window needs.
- The section grows with 50% headroom (a widening drag at 12 px a step stays in
  one section for ~58 steps) and is given back once the window is a quarter of
  it, so one maximize does not pin its memory for good.
