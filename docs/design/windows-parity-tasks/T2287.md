# T2287 - A Windows card going into its strip should narrow into the strip rather than slide away full size and vanish (geometry half of T2269, Mac a81db68f9)

## In plain terms

When a pane gets folded away into a narrow strip at the edge of a row (by
dragging a divider, or by bringing another pane forward), Windows used to slide
the whole full-size pane off to the edge and then make it vanish, with the
strip popping in afterwards. Now the pane's card visibly narrows into its strip
- its contents slide behind the strip's edge - and coming back out it widens
out of the strip again, the way it looks on Mac.

## Goals

- [x] a folding pane's card narrows into its strip instead of sliding off full size
- [x] an unfolding pane's card widens out of its strip
- [x] the terminal is never resized mid-glide (its window is held and cut to the card)
- [x] the strip appears only once the card has reached it

## Summary

Mac a81db68f9 geometry half of T2269. A tucked card's glide target becomes its STRIP rect (Placement.tab), not its held rect at the slot: the card narrows into the strip. The pane window stays at its held size (stack_held), positioned so its far edge rides the drawn card's far edge (Mac glideAnchor: strip leads -> anchor trailing/bottom, else leading/top), and is cut to the drawn card by its window region (slideClip/applyCardRegions already cut to cardRect - extend with an x/y offset for the anchor). The desk paints that card's sheet at the drawn rect (deskSlots) and suppresses its band in tuckedStrips until the glide reaches it (Slide.rect(id) == null). Coming out is the reverse: from the strip rect, widening, content anchored to the far edge. Pure logic in pane_stack_slide.zig: a target(Placed) helper (strip rect for tucked), an anchor offset, and collapse(drawn, peek_px, span_px) = clamp(1 - (min(w,h) - peek)/span) with Mac peek 36 DIP, fadeSpan 120 DIP - unit-tested. Acceptance: extend test/win32/stack-fold-slide.ps1 (GHOZTTY_STACK_SLIDE_MS stretch) to sample a folding card mid-glide: its visible width shrinks toward the strip width and no full-size card is left at the slot.
