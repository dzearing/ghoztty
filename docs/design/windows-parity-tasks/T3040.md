# T3040 - PA-V08: the contents card's slide animation creates a D3D11 and DirectComposition device that the viewer then keeps for its whole life (viewer_toc_comp.zig:123)

## Summary

PA-V08 (audit-1, T2963). viewer_toc_comp.zig:123-168 creates a D3D11 device, a DComp device, a target and two visuals on the first compositor slide (ViewerTOCPanel.zig:1180); finishCompSlide and cancelCompSlide only call host.stop (:1271, :1285); release is in destroy (:404-405). Cost (estimated by analogy with PA-R02: driver threads and GPU memory per device): one device per viewer that ever slid its card. Fix: deinit the host when the slide ends, or share PA-R02's device. Risk: low; the next slide recreates it, the stepped path is the fallback. Findings: docs/design/perf-audit/audit-1/V-findings.md.

## In plain terms

In a narrow markdown viewer, the contents card slides in and out. The first time it did, the viewer quietly set up a whole graphics device of its own (dozens of threads and several MB of video memory) and kept it until the viewer was closed, even though the slide only takes a fraction of a second. Now the slide borrows the graphics device the app already has and gives back everything else as soon as the card stops moving.

## Goals

- [x] the slide draws on the app's one shared graphics device instead of creating its own
- [x] everything else the slide built (the compositor device, target, picture) is released when the slide ends or is cancelled
- [x] the slide still runs on the compositor, and still falls back to stepping if it cannot
