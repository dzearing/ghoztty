# T2741 - Composition step 4d-2: in pane-tree mode each card's shadow is a compositor visual just under its pane visual, placed and glided in the same commit (panePlace/paneGlide carry it), re-rendered only when the card's size or shadow share changes

## Summary

Second split of T2511: the shadow visual that moves with its card, including through a declared glide while the UI thread is stalled.

Inputs from T2740 (`src/apprt/win32/shadow_layer.zig`): `render` gives the
card's premultiplied layer, `Layer.origin` where it goes, `Key` when it must be
re-rendered (size, share, style - never position). Two design points it left
open for this step:

- **Keep a layer off its neighbours.** On the desk no shadow lands on another
  card (fills go down after every shadow); a layer under each pane visual would
  darken a neighbouring card's rim and edge wherever the reach crosses the gap.
  Clip each layer away from neighbouring cards, or carry the rims up with the
  pane visuals - decide here, and prove it with a WGC capture of two cards
  closer than the reach.
- **A fading share as opacity.** A card gliding into its strip changes its
  share every frame; `render(s)` vs `render(1)` at opacity `s` is within 4
  alpha levels (T2740's probe), so fade the visual's opacity (declarable, like
  the bell fade) instead of re-rendering.

## In plain terms

Every pane card throws a soft shadow onto the coloured desk around it. Until
now that shadow was painted into the desk picture, so when cards slid (a
pane stack gliding to a new pane) the shadow could only follow if the whole
desk was repainted, and while the app was busy it stayed behind. Now, with
the new compositor path switched on, each card's shadow is a little picture
of its own that the system compositor moves together with the card, in the
same step. On screen it looks exactly as before; the difference is that it
keeps up with its card even while the app is busy.

## Goals

- [x] Each card's shadow is its own picture on the compositor, laid under the panes, the focus glow and anything drawn over the desk
- [x] The desk is drawn without that card's shadow, so it is never there twice
- [x] It moves with its card in the same step, at rest and through a glide the compositor runs while the app's thread is stalled
- [x] It never darkens a neighbouring card's edge: it is kept off every other card, as the painted desk always was
- [x] It is redrawn only when the card's size, the style, or a neighbour's place beside it changes; a card fading its shadow into its strip just fades it

## Design (2026-11-07)

- **Where the visuals hang.** Not in the pane layer: it is a separate topmost
  target, so a shadow there would darken the tab strip (the desk visual's clip
  stops below the caption) and lie OVER the focus glow, which the desk lays
  over its shadows. Instead the host makes a **shadow layer** as the desk
  visual's first child when the desk goes up (`Host.shadow_root`), so the desk's
  clip applies, every later child (glow, overlay, hero slide) lies over it,
  and every pane visual (the topmost target) lies over all of it - a shadow can
  never darken a pane's own pixels. One DComp device commits both targets, so
  the shadow still moves in the pane's own commit.
- **Riding.** `chrome_comp.ShadowVis` (visual + effect group + surface, tagged
  with the `desk_gen` it hangs under) is owned by the pane's `Surface`.
  `panePlace` / `paneGlide` take a `ShadowRide` (`dx`, `dy` from the pane
  visual's offset to the layer's, the desk origin folded in): a place sets the
  shadow's offset in the same commit, a glide animates it along the same cubics
  from `from + d` to `to + d`, so it tracks the card exactly. `dx`/`dy` are
  read at rest and kept through a glide.
- **Neighbours: clipped away, not rims carried up** (the open point T2740
  left). `shadow_layer.Key.masked` records every other card in reach relative
  to this one (`Mask`, up to 8), and `renderKey` clears the layer under each by
  its coverage, exactly as it clears under its own card. Carrying the rims up
  with the pane visuals would have meant the pane's swap chain painting desk
  pixels it does not own. A pair that moves together keeps its layers; during
  a glide the layer keeps the mask it had (no per-frame renders) and the paint
  that lands it renders the true one.
- **A share is an opacity.** The layer is rendered at share 1 and the effect
  group's opacity is the card's share (T2740's probe: within 4 levels), so a
  card trading its shadow for its strip renders nothing per frame.
- **Desk side.** `Window.syncTreeShadows` runs in the desk paint, before the
  compose: each tree pane in the paint's slots puts its shadow up and its card
  is composed with `shadow = 0`; every other pane of the window takes its down.
  Only over a desk on the compositor with nothing GDI-drawn over it (no
  rearrange header band, no hero split, no lit gutter bar - the compose lays
  the bar over every shadow) - otherwise everything stays baked. The tucked
  strips' bands are laid after the cards' shadows too, so they join the mask
  with the other cards (found in self-review: a riding shadow would otherwise
  darken a strip next to its card).
  Any failed step logs `pane shadow visual failed at <stage>` and that card's
  shadow goes back into the compose. `GHOZTTY_COMP_SHADOWS=0` keeps every
  shadow baked (default on, inside the opt-in pane-tree mode); debug knobs
  `GHOZTTY_COMP_SHADOWS_DOUBLE` / `_NOMASK` are the oracle's controls.
- Left for T2742: the desk still recomposes per glide step (now without the
  riding cards' shadows); the zero-recompose counter and a film of the shadow
  tracking its card are its oracle. Blade shadows stay T2743.
