# T2120 - Stacked panes: dragging a tucked pane's strip picks the pane up (Mac PaneStackTabView blade drag: the card lifts out of its stack, drops into a row slot, a window edge or a screen)

## Summary

T2128 landed the pure half: `pane_drop.bladeSlots` / `resolveBlade` /
`tileFrame` (the `.reorder` and `.tile` targets) and their previews in
`drop_highlight.forTarget` (`.reorder` caret, `.tile` as a new-window
footprint). `Window.ownedDrop` maps both to null today. What is left here:
start a strip drag on a press-and-move (a press without movement stays T2109's
bring-forward), build the row's `RowItem`s and every monitor's work area into a
`BladeDrag`, resolve through `resolveBlade`, and COMMIT `.reorder` (move the
pane beside the named item within its row, staying a strip) and `.tile` (a new
window over that half of the screen). Acceptance: a strip dropped between two
cards lands there; a strip over a card's middle does nothing.

T2109 made a strip click bring the pane forward; Mac's strip is also a drag source (PaneDragSourceView isBlade, PaneDropResolver.resolveBlade). On Windows a press on a strip is a click only. Wire a strip press into the rearrange drag (beginRearrangeDrag) with blade targets.

## In plain terms

When a row of panes is too narrow and some panes fold away into thin strips,
a strip on Windows could only be clicked. On the Mac you can also pick it up
and drag it: drop it between two other panes of that row to move it there, or
against the edge of the screen to give it a window of its own over that half
of the screen. Now Windows does the same; a plain click still brings the pane
forward.

## Goals

- [x] a press on a strip that travels past the drag threshold picks the pane up; one that does not is still the click
- [x] the drag resolves to a strip's targets (row slots, screen edges, sidebar, window edge), with their previews
- [x] a drop in a row slot moves the pane beside that item (a column is matched by all its panes)
- [x] a drop at a screen edge opens a window over that half of the screen holding the pane
- [x] a drop over the middle of a card does nothing
