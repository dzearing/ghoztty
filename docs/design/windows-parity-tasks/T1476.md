# T1476 - Dragging a split divider blinks the window through to the desktop frame

## Summary

**Reported interactively by the user, 2026-09-09.** With Claude Code running in
the left pane of a two-pane split, dragging the divider to resize makes the
window blink occasionally during the drag — and what shows through the blink is
a clean, empty window frame, i.e. the desktop/background rather than terminal
content.

The user's own reading of it, which is the most useful lead here: it looks like
the resize path is *painting directly to the screen* rather than composing a
frame off-screen and blitting the finished result. A blink that reveals the bare
frame is the signature of the window being erased (or a new, larger client area
being exposed) and then painted a beat later, with the gap visible to the eye.
A double-buffered path cannot show that intermediate state, because the erased
surface is never the one on screen.

Places to look, in order:

- **`WM_ERASEBKGND`.** Letting `DefWindowProc` erase with the class background
  brush during a live drag is the classic cause of exactly this. A window that
  paints every pixel of its client area should answer it with 1 and erase
  nothing.
- **The live-resize paint path.** During a modal drag loop, Windows can send
  paints faster than a full re-layout + re-render completes; if the pane content
  is rendered in more than one step, the partly-painted intermediate is what
  blinks.
- **Whether the divider drag re-creates or re-sizes the child surfaces.** A
  surface destroyed and re-made mid-drag has, by definition, a frame where it
  has nothing to show.
- **DWM/composition settings on the render target** — a swapchain resize
  discards its buffers, and whether that discarded state is ever presented.

Not yet root-caused; the above is the hypothesis stack, not a finding. Note the
report says *occasionally*, so whatever the cause, it is timing-dependent and a
single clean drag is not evidence of a fix.

Mac parity note: the Mac build composes through AppKit/Core Animation and does
not show this, so this is a Windows-side rendering defect rather than a shared
one — but confirm that on the Mac seat rather than assuming it.
