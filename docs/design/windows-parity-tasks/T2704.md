# T2704 - Dropping a dragged pane on a divider does nothing on Windows; Mac now inserts the pane in that gap with a third of the split space

## In plain terms

On the Mac you can now drop a pane right onto the line between two panes, and
it slots in between them. On Windows letting go over that line does nothing.
After this lands, the divider is a drop target on Windows too.

## Goals

- [x] Releasing a dragged pane on a divider puts it in that gap (same tab, another tab, another window)
- [x] It takes a third of the divider's split; the two sides give up their share equally
- [x] The drop is previewed as the landing area with a seam down each side

## Summary

main intake 2026-11-04, from 30d13ea14. Mac's new row in the drop table:

| Where released | Result |
|---|---|
| A **divider** (10pt either side of the gap's middle) | **Insert** in its gap, spanning its length, with a third of the space its split had; the two sides each give up their share |

- Precedence: the window edge band beats a divider, and a divider beats the
  pane under it.
- A divider hides between panes overlapping in a stack - there is no gap to
  drop in (`PaneDropCandidate.Divider.all`).
- The hover preview fills the insert area with seams where the new dividers run.

Windows today: `src/apprt/win32/pane_drop.zig` resolves "inside the window but
between panes" to nothing (test "a gap between panes is a divider and resolves
to nothing"). Port the resolver rule (pure, unit-tested in the `none` lane)
and the tree insert, plus the preview.

## Notes

- Mechanism: pure `pane_drop.dividerFor` + `.insert` target; `pane_gap.zig` walks the tree in screen order, so a split's sides are two contiguous runs of one id buffer (no allocation per mouse move); `SplitTree.insertInGap`; seams drawn in the on-accent frame brush because the preview is one layered window with a single alpha (Mac draws accent seams over a translucent accent fill - same-color seams would vanish here).
- The preview's landing is a third of the divider's split as drawn BEFORE the dragged pane leaves; when the dragged pane is itself inside that split the committed width is a third of the post-removal split. That is Mac's behavior too (its landing is computed from the same pre-removal rects).
- Not covered here: a divider beside the dragged pane with nothing else on its side previews an insert and then does nothing - that is T2705's "a spot that would leave the pane where it is doesn't react".
