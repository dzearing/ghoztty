# T2516 - Window-edge resize paints every surface on its own clock: the viewer (WebView2) pane lands offset from its chrome and overlaps the terminal, regions show blank desk, and the viewer is clipped past the frame (user screenshots 2026-10-30). Every frame of a window resize must show chrome, terminal and viewer at the same geometry

## Summary

The user, 2026-10-30 ~10:00, on their installed build (CLI reports 1.50.9+f050e072b):
"here's a few ugly frames showing how our painting is completely out of sync on
resize. see misalligned web browser pane and blank areas".

Screenshots (window: controller terminal on the left, tracker viewer on the right):

- `docs/design/resize-sync/user-2026-10-30-window-resize-viewer-offset.png`: the
  viewer's page content starts ~55 px LEFT of its own address bar and paints over
  the terminal's right edge. The WebView2 child is at an old position while the
  chrome is already at the new one.
- `docs/design/resize-sync/user-2026-10-30-window-resize-blank-clipped.png`: the
  terminal column shows a blank desk strip, and the viewer page runs past the
  window's right edge and is clipped. Again the children are at different
  geometries in the same frame.

So during a window-edge resize the frame, the chrome (desk, address bar, banner),
the terminal pane HWND and the WebView2 controller each move and present on their
own schedule. The Mac moves all of them in one layout transaction.

Find out, per surface, when its new bounds land relative to the frame's
WM_SIZE/WM_WINDOWPOSCHANGED: the WebView2 `put_Bounds`, the terminal
SetWindowPos (or DeferWindowPos), and the desk paint. Then put them into one
transaction: a single DeferWindowPos batch for the child HWNDs, the WebView2
bounds set inside the same pass (or its parent HWND moved in the batch, with the
controller bounds fixed relative to it), and the desk painted in that pass. A
surface that cannot have its new pixels ready must hold its old content at its
NEW position (stretched or clipped), never paint at an old position or leave blank
desk. Relates: T1392 (viewer address bar a frame late, done), T2503/T2506
(resize-flicker harness), T2418 (divider drag smear, the third screenshot).

## In plain terms

When you drag the edge of a Ghoztty window that has a terminal beside a web
page, the picture tears for a moment on every step: patches of plain
background colour flash around the panes, and for one frame the page sits in
its new place while everything else is still where it was a step ago, so the
terminal runs under the page and the page is cut off at the window edge. When
this is done, every frame of the drag shows one consistent layout.

## Goals

- [x] a way to film every frame of a resize without disturbing the person at the desk (`scripts\resize-sync-onscreen.ps1`)
- [x] no frame shows plain background where the desk belongs (the "blank areas")
- [ ] the window's own content lands in the same frame as the window's new size and the page (the "misaligned web browser pane") - timed and split 2026-10-30: T2520, T2521, T2522

## Findings (2026-10-30)

Filmed with `scripts\resize-sync-onscreen.ps1` (new): a ghost window
(`GHOZTTY_DEBUG_GHOST=bottom`) with a green-background terminal split right
with a solid magenta viewer page, resized 30 steps of 12 px inside a
WM_ENTERSIZEMOVE/EXITSIZEMOVE pair, every DWM frame captured by
`ghoztty-comp-spike.exe --film-hwnd` (new: whole-window WGC film, pool sized
for the largest frame, each frame cropped to its ContentSize). WGC only emits
a frame on change, so there are 2-5 frames per step.

Two defects, one per frame shape:

1. **Flat background round the cards** (the user's "blank areas"): the first
   frame of each step had the window's flat background in every gutter and
   margin. Source: `clearVacatedPaneStrips` (T2418) fills what the panes
   vacated with the background and the desk paint lands a separate GDI flush
   later, after `cutCardRegions` and `placePaneOverlays`; DWM composed between
   them. **Fixed**: in the elevated style the vacated strips are painted with
   the desk itself (regions cut first, so the corners they give back are
   covered), and the pass's closing paint blits whole because the strips'
   paint already composed the desk. Film: flat-background frames 50/50/48/55
   per drag before, **0/0/0/0** after.
2. **Content one compose behind the frame** (screenshot 2, and screenshot 1's
   page beside its bar): for one frame per step DWM shows the NEW window size
   with the window's own content - desk, cards, the viewer's address bar, the
   terminal, the caption buttons - at the PREVIOUS step's layout, clipped by
   the new frame, while the WebView2 page (its own composition surface) is
   already at the new geometry. Still open; this is the remaining work. The
   layout and desk paint run synchronously inside WM_SIZE, so the frame size
   is being shown before that pass's GDI writes and GL presents reach the
   compositor. Next: time the frame's size change against the pass's flushes
   (QPC stamps in `handleResize`) and look at holding the frame until the pass
   is done (DWM resize sync for the redirection surface, or moving the desk to
   the composition path T2468 is building, with the WebView2 bounds and the
   pane presents in the same commit).

### Defect 2 timed (2026-10-30, second turn)

`GHOZTTY_RESIZE_TRACE=1` (new) logs a QPC stamp per top-level message and per
phase of the layout pass; the film's `frames.txt` holds each compose's QPC
time, so the two line up frame by frame (`resize-sync-onscreen.ps1` header).

- **The old harness overstated it.** A cross-process `SetWindowPos` puts the
  new frame on screen ~14 ms after `WM_NCCALCSIZE` but only sends
  `WM_WINDOWPOSCHANGED`/`WM_SIZE` ~20 ms after it. A real edge drag runs the
  size loop on the window's own thread, so the harness now posts each step
  there (`SWP_ASYNCWINDOWPOS`; `-CrossThread` keeps the old shape). There
  `WM_SIZE` lands ~1 ms after `WM_NCCALCSIZE`.
- **What is left is the pass's length against the compositor.** DWM shows the
  new frame size at the first compose after the `SetWindowPos`, whatever the
  window has painted by then. The pass takes ~15 ms at ReleaseSafe (the ghost
  now runs in ReleaseSafe for this), ~23 ms Debug, so at 120 Hz the new frame
  always goes up with the previous step's content. The renderer wait is NOT
  the cost (0.2 ms median). Per pass, ReleaseSafe medians:
  pane batch (terminal reflow + viewer) 5.3 ms, of which viewer 1.8 ms (nav bar
  1.5 ms); card regions 1.5 ms; desk fill of the vacated strips 7.1 ms, of which
  rebuilding the desk at the new size 3.6 ms; overlays + band paint 1.1 ms.
- **Split:** T2520 (desk rebuilt every step, P0), T2521 (nav bar placement,
  P1), T2522 (pace each step to a compose once the pass fits a frame, P1). Even
  all three only make the stale frame rarer; the complete fix is every surface
  in one composition commit (T2508-T2511).
