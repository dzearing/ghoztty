# T3706 - Linux L11b6: undo puts back a pane drag - an in-tab, cross-tab or cross-window drop is one undoable step, as on the Mac

## Summary

Follow-up of T3616. Every pane move on the Mac goes through PaneMoveCoordinator.commit / crossWindowCommit with undoAction 'Move Pane', grouped across the two controllers, so Undo (Cmd+Z) puts the pane back - including a window the move emptied and closed. On GTK SplitTree.dropPaneAt, dropArriving and Window.dropOnTabBar/adoptFromWindow call SplitTree.rearrange directly, so no drag is undoable, and a window a drag empties is destroyed outright (CloseUndo never parks it). Port: record the move on src/apprt/gtk/CloseUndo.zig (both trees, the tab index, and the emptied window to reopen), undo/redo it, and extend test/linux/cli-pane-drag.sh and cli-undo.sh.

## In plain terms

On Linux, dragging a pane somewhere (inside its tab, to another tab, onto the
tab bar, into another window, or out into a window of its own) could not be
undone: Ctrl+Shift+Z did nothing, and a window the drag emptied was simply
gone. Now every drag is one undo step, as on the Mac: undo puts the pane back
exactly where it was and reopens a tab or window the drag emptied; redo does
the drag again.

## Goals

- [x] a drop inside the pane's own tab is one undo and one redo
- [x] a drop on the tab bar (a new tab) undoes: the new tab goes, the pane goes home; redo makes the tab again
- [x] a drop from another tab that empties its tab undoes: that tab comes back with the pane
- [x] a drop into another window undoes from that window
- [x] a window's last pane joining another window: undo brings the emptied window back, redo closes it again
- [x] a pop-out into a new window: undo closes that window and puts the pane back, redo pops it out again
- [x] every move keeps the same panes (same shells, same pids)
- [ ] a lone pane's tab reordered on the tab bar is undoable (filed as T3840)
