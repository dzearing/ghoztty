# T2778 - Composition step 5c-4b: a folding card's whole path is declared to DirectComposition - its card clip and its carried frame's place on the 45 ms follower, and the content's fade as a curve along it - so a stalled window thread no longer freezes the folding card while its neighbours glide on

## Summary

Split of T2640, after 5c-4a. Once the face is a visual, declare it the way T2572 declared the cards beside it: the card edge's follower (comp_overlay.fitFollower) drives the face container's clip edges and the carried picture's offset, and the content opacity (strip_face.tweenCollapse of the card's extent, which is linear in the extent inside the zone) is fitted as its own 0..1 curve along the same follower. The per-frame fade limit (T2442, Slide.limitFade) exists for SLOW window-thread frames; on the compositor's clock every frame is a display frame (8-16 ms), where the limit never bites (the 60 fps test already proves that), so the declared path is the bare follower - record that. A retarget re-declares from the reached value (TreeGlide.reached). The folding card's pane visual itself is hidden under a carried face, so only the face is declared. Oracle: comp-glide-hold-onscreen.ps1 -Arms stall scoring the folding card's face (longest still stretch within one frame through a 500 ms GHOZTTY_DEBUG_GLIDE_STALL_MS stall), -NoDeclare red as the negative control; the T2485 chain-fold film 0 bad frames on an idle box.

## In plain terms

When a pane folds away into its strip, the strip's title card covers it and
the pane's picture fades out under it as it narrows. In the new composition
mode the app used to work out every frame of that fold itself, so if the app
got busy for a moment the folding pane froze while the panes beside it kept
gliding. Now the app tells Windows the whole fold once - where the card edges
go, where the pane's picture rides, and how it fades - and Windows plays it,
so the fold keeps moving through a busy moment too. Nothing changes in the
default mode.

## Goals

- [x] the folding card's face is three pictures in the window's tree - the band, the strip's name, the carried frame at its own strength - under one container cut to the card
- [x] the card's edges, the frame's place, the name's place and the frame's strength all run on the glide's 45 ms follower, declared once on the face's first frame
- [x] the fade is a straight line along the same follower and is held where it runs out (a strip narrower than the fade's end), so it never goes past 0
- [x] a drag that moves the landing re-declares from where the compositor has got to
- [x] `GHOZTTY_GLIDE_DECLARE=0`, `GHOZTTY_FACE_COMP=0`, forced slow frames and a card opening out of its strip stay on frames

## Decisions made here

- **No per-frame fade limit on the declared path.** T2442's `limitFade` exists for SLOW window-thread frames; on the compositor's clock every frame is a display frame (8-16 ms), where it never bites. A test's forced slow frames (`GHOZTTY_STACK_SLIDE_DT_MS`) keep the face on frames, so stack-fold-slide's slow arm still sees the limit.
- **The band never moves.** It is painted once over everywhere the face goes on the way (the box of where it starts and where it lands), so its glass is the desk's own pixel under it, and the card clip cuts it.
- **The name is its own picture**, ink at the coverage GDI drew, riding the face's centre across its narrowing side (where `labelOrigin` centres it); its length along the strip does not change on a fold, so its cut does not either.
- **Unfold stays on frames.** An opening card's frame is still being drawn while it widens (T2458); only a fold carries a frame that is fixed for the whole glide.
