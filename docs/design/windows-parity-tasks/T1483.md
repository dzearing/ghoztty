# T1483 - The watchdog diagnosed a 63-hour stall 716 times and never acted, because its rearm gate can be starved

## Summary

**The loop did no work between 2026-09-09 09:46 and 2026-09-12 01:11 - 63.5
hours - while the watchdog watched it, named the stall correctly every five
minutes, and declined to act every time.** This is not a missed detection. The
detector was right 716 times; the thing wired downstream of it never fired.

The numbers, from `%TEMP%\ghoztty-go-loop-watchdog.log` over the gap:

- **716** `STALLED(by=turn)` detections, climbing from `turn_age=604.8m` to
  `turn_age=3,804.5m` against a 180m limit.
- **536** of those ended in `stale (held) but rearm not elapsed (N < 20m);
  waiting`.
- The `N` in that line is `age(by=transcript)` - how recently the PANE emitted
  anything - and it kept landing at 5m, 10m, 15m: under the 20m rearm threshold,
  over and over, for two and a half days.

So the rearm gate is **starvable**. Any pane that emits something more often
than every 20 minutes holds it shut forever, no matter how far past its limit
the turn clock is. A stall of 3,804 minutes and a stall of 181 minutes are
treated identically the moment the pane coughs. The gate exists to stop the
watchdog re-entering too eagerly, and its input is a signal the stalled session
itself keeps producing.

**What finally broke it** was luck, not design. At `01:11:03` the transcript age
happened to reach `20.1m` - four seconds of slack over the threshold - and the
watchdog acted immediately and correctly:

```
01:11:03 STALLED(by=turn) ... age=20.1m turn_age=3,804.5m
01:11:03 pane ... occupant=claude (lock owner_alive=True)
01:11:24 re-entering: nudge live session in pane ... (state=held, occupant=claude)
01:11:24   wiped composer exit=0
01:11:24   send-keys exit=0
01:11:28   NUDGE SUBMITTED: the composer emptied
01:16:29 healthy: ... turn_age=0.5m session=working
```

Five minutes later the loop was working, and in the next 90 minutes it closed
T1476, T1478 and T1481. The recovery path is not broken. Only the permission to
take it is.

**A second defect made this invisible from the outside**, and it is the reason
nobody noticed for three days. `go-loop-health.ps1` reported
`HEALTHY uptime=2d 16h 56m` throughout. `uptime` is measured from the lock's
`acquired` timestamp, which never moved because the lock was never released -
so it measures *how long the lock has been held*, not *how long work has been
getting done*. The one field `start-go` tells a supervisor to trust ("uptime is
the signal that matters ... `alive` alone cannot tell a loop that has run all
night from one revived forty seconds ago") is precisely the field that cannot
tell a working loop from a wedged one. During the gap, `turn=1` sat unchanged
and `turn_age` was in the thousands of minutes; both were visible and both were
right. The headline was the only thing that lied.

Note also `composer=pending` on the early detections (2026-09-09 19:51): the
session was sitting on unsent text, the exact shape `go-loop-health.ps1`'s own
comment at :368 records from a previous incident ("the watchdog logged
STALLED(by=composer) over a composer holding unsent text while this line, six
feet away, printed HEALTHY - once for 76 minutes with the turn's work
uncommitted"). That was filed and fixed for the composer clock; the turn clock
has the same hole.

Fixes, in the order they matter:

- **The rearm gate must not be starvable by the stalled session.** Escalate it
  against `turn_age`: once the turn clock is some multiple past its limit, the
  pane's chatter stops being a reason to wait. A gate whose input the subject
  controls is not a gate.
- **`uptime` must describe work, not lock tenure.** Either derive it from the
  last completed turn, or print both and let the smaller one set the verdict. A
  loop with `turn_age=3,804m` must never print `HEALTHY`.
- Consider whether `STALLED` repeated N times without an action should itself
  escalate - 536 identical refusals in a row is a signal no one was reading.

## Validation criteria

- [ ] A pane that emits output every few minutes while its turn clock runs past
      the limit is nudged anyway; the rearm gate cannot be held shut by pane
      activity alone.
- [ ] `go-loop-health.ps1` never prints `HEALTHY` while `turn_age` exceeds the
      watchdog's stall limit; the verdict reflects the turn clock, and `uptime`
      either measures productive time or is shown beside the figure that does.
- [ ] An acceptance script in `test\win32\` stages a held lock with a stale turn
      clock and a pane that keeps emitting, and asserts both the nudge fires and
      health reports the stall - with a negative control that goes red against
      today's watchdog and today's health line.
- [ ] The 716/536 counts above are reproduced by the harness as a regression
      fixture, so the starvation shape is asserted rather than remembered.
