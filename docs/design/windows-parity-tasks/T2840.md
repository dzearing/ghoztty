# T2840 - Blade layers on glass: a glass stack's strips keep baking their blade shadows because blade_layer's mask is proven against solid bands only - prove it against paintGlassBand (unit + comp-blade-onscreen glass arm) and drop the !glass gate

## Summary

T2796 put each resting strip's blade shadow on the compositor
(`blade_rides.zig`), but `blade_rides.ridable` refuses a band with `glass`
set and `Window.paintDesk` passes `!glass` into the eligibility: a glass
band is `paintGlassBand` - the card's sheet over the BARE desk under each
pixel, edged with a rim - and `blade_layer`'s mask (cleared by
`bandCoverage` at `bandRadius`) has only been held against `paintBand`
(T2743's tests). So on glass the desk still bakes every blade.

Fix: a `blade_layer` unit test composing glass bands both ways (bake vs
shadowless + layers) within T2743's bounds - the rim and the sheet's read of
the desk are where it can differ - then drop the gate, and add `g` arms
(`--macos-pane-glass=true`) to `scripts\comp-blade-onscreen.ps1`.

## In plain terms

With see-through ("glass") panes, the small shadows between folded strips
are still painted into the background picture. This moves them onto the
compositor the way solid panes' already are. Nothing should look different.

## Validation criteria

- [ ] `blade_layer` unit test over glass bands, within T2743's bounds
- [ ] `comp-blade-onscreen.ps1` glass arms match their baked base; `-NegativeControl` still red
