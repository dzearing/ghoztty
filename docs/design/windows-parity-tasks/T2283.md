# T2283 - Windows strips sit apart with a gutter between each; Mac's collapsed panes are now blades - no gap between strips, each tucked 12pt under the next, with a tight shadow that leans outward and fades in with the collapse (Mac 5a9a2e2a4, afc64dc53)

## Summary

Mac 5a9a2e2a4: strips lie side by side with no gap (Metrics.bladeGap = 0), each casting a tight, stronger shadow on the next one out; the gutter stays only between the run of panes on show and each group of strips, where the pull-back grip lives. The pure layout and the sidebar divider-tuck use the blade extent. afc64dc53: a strip with another between it and the run reaches 12pt UNDER that one (bladeTuck), its rounded edge hidden; titles centre on the visible part; the tuck still counts as fully collapsed. The blade shadow leans away from the run, stops at the strip's sides, and follows the collapse fraction so it fades in during the glide. Windows: pane_stack_layout.zig strips with gutters. Depends on T2269 (card narrows into its strip).

## In plain terms

When a row of panes has more than fits, the ones that do not fit fold into thin strips at the edge. On Windows those strips sat apart with a gap between each, like little cards. On Mac they now lie flush, each tucked a little under the next like a fanned stack of blades, with the gap kept only between the strips and the panes you are looking at. Windows now lays them out the same way; the soft shadow each blade casts on the next is its own follow-up (T2307).
