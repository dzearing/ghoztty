# T737 - A tab's width can still change under a stationary pointer (T249 residual)

## In plain terms

Tabs are sized to their labels, and the label of a tab running a command is
the command. T249 stopped the tab snapping back when the command finishes, so
the strip no longer twitches all day — but the outward growth is still there,
and it can still slide the tab you were about to click out from under your
mouse. Rare now, and it only happens as something starts, but it is still a
click landing on the wrong tab.

## Summary

T249 measured the defect (one `ping` moved tab 2 and the "+" ±186 px) and
shipped a grow-only ratchet: a tab widens the moment its title needs the room
and narrows only at a structural relayout. That deleted the shrink half of the
motion — the half that fires minutes later, caused by nothing the user did.

The grow half remains by design: refusing it would ellipsize a title with strip
to spare, which is exactly what T235 exists to prevent. But the grow can still
be unattributable — a *background* tab whose build finishes, or whose command
starts, widens that tab and slides every tab right of it. If the pointer is
resting over one of those, the click lands somewhere else.

The candidate fix, named in D67's mitigation: **freeze the tab run's widths
while the pointer is inside the strip**, re-fitting when it leaves. Widths then
never change while anyone is pointing at them, which makes the wrong-tab hit
structurally impossible rather than merely rare. The cost is that a tab needing
more room stays ellipsized while you hover — a small, self-correcting,
user-attributable state.

Establish it is worth building before building it. `hover_tab` only tracks the
pointer being over a TAB, so "inside the strip" needs a real mouse-leave-tracked
flag covering the empty run and the "+" too; that is the actual work, and it is
not obviously worth it for a hazard that now requires a background pane to
retitle at the moment your hand is still.
