# T2537 - A window-edge resize step's compose is ~1.8 ms of pure card painting (ReleaseSafe, no page faults left after T2529): the desk copy plus every card's interior, shadow and rim is redone over the whole window at each new size

## Summary

Measured by T2529 (resize-sync-onscreen.ps1, zig-out-rsafe, GHOZTTY_RESIZE_TRACE, which now splits rest_us into compose_us/lights_us/names_us/blit_us): with comp over a kept section the compose takes ~5 page faults per step yet still costs 1.77 ms median (p90 2.1 ms), blit 0.24 ms. It is compute: compose() memcpys the 4 MB desk and pane_elevation.paintCardsWith fills every card interior per pixel (glass LUTs), plus shadows/glows/rims, for the whole window on every size. Ideas: on a size change only the cards' edges and the newly exposed desk move, so a resize could keep the interiors (cards resized by the same delta, terminal/viewer fills are flat or LUT-only), or SIMD the interior fill and desk copy into one pass. Validate with the same trace (compose_us down materially) and pane-elevation.ps1 green. Blocks T2516 (the 8.3 ms per-step budget).

## In plain terms

While you drag a window's edge, every new size repaints the panes' cards
(their rounded corners, rims, shadows and focus glow) from scratch, and that
repaint was the biggest piece of each step. Most of it was being worked out
pixel by pixel for rows where every pixel gets the same answer. It now works
those out once per row and lays them eight pixels at a time, and the picture
comes out exactly the same. Each resize step's repaint is about a quarter
cheaper.

## Goals

- [x] find where the ~1.8 ms actually goes (it was not the interior fills)
- [x] cut it without changing a single pixel of the paint
- [x] the remainder measured and filed (T2539)

## Findings

Micro-bench, 1500x800 at 1.5x, ReleaseSafe, a glass terminal beside a viewer
with a glass nav bar (`paintCardsWith` alone; the desk copy is a further
~150 us either way):

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| glass + glow + shadows | 1473 us | 741 us |
| solid + glow + shadows | 1133 us | 468 us |
| solid, no glow/shadows | 602 us | 165 us |
| glass, no glow/shadows | 950 us | 443 us |

- The task's premise was only partly true. The solid interiors are already a
  `memset`, and the glass interior's four table reads cost ~0.45 ns a pixel.
  The cost was in the scalar float `blend` of rows with a single answer per
  row. On a card's rounded top and bottom rows, every pixel between the two
  corners is one distance from the edge, and the same holds for the glow's
  bands above and below the card. The cards' rounded rows alone cost 600 us
  on solid cards, and the glow ~370 us.
- Fix (all in `pane_elevation.zig`): `Frame.flatSpan` finds that run, as the
  per-pixel test reads it. `blendSpan`, `blendEach` and `GlassLut.edgeSpan` lay
  it eight lanes at a time with the same operations in the same order, so the
  bits are the same. `GlassLut.under` composes the glass head's material over
  the sheet into one table: four reads a pixel where it was eight.
- Interiors were not kept across sizes: the desk is stretched to the window,
  so every glass pixel's backdrop changes at every size.

In the app, A/B with only `pane_elevation.zig` differing (HEAD in
`zig-out-t2537base`, this change in `zig-out-rsafe`), resize-sync-onscreen.ps1
with GHOZTTY_RESIZE_TRACE, 127 resize steps each:

| | compose_us median | p90 | rest_us median | blit_us |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| before | 1668 | 1954 | 1863 | 201 |
| after | 1229 | 1466 | 1430 | 197 |
