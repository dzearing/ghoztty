# T2388 - FrameScore's B6 blend rule only accepts straight sRGB blends, but the renderer antialiases text in linear light, so any real text in a lab scene (tab titles, sidebar labels, a prompt) reads as thousands of garbage pixels

## Summary

Found by T2383 section D on the real app: a lab card whose busy text was ring-coloured on Bg scored 14,039 B6 pixels from text that was rendering correctly - the renderer's glyph edges lie on a curve between the two colours, not the straight line OnAnyBlend tests (Tol 8). T2383 sidestepped it for the card (busy text is drawn Bg-on-Bg), but T2384's scenes include chrome with real text (tab strip, sidebar, banner) and DWM's shadow bands. Fix: accept blends along the linear-light curve (and gamma-2.2 for GDI/DWM) as well as the sRGB line, proved on a capture of real text through capture-pane, with the negative kept: a colour off every curve is still garbage.

## In plain terms

The frame lab judges every pixel of a captured frame, and a pixel that is not
one of the scene's colours or a smooth mix of two of them counts as "garbage".
Its idea of a smooth mix was too narrow: the terminal draws the soft edges of
letters with a different (more accurate) kind of mixing, so any ordinary text
on screen was being reported as thousands of garbage pixels. After this, real
text reads as clean, and genuinely wrong colours are still caught.
