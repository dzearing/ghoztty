# T1905 - On a translucent Windows window (background-opacity below 1) the pane sidebar is GDI-painted into a per-pixel-alpha parent with alpha 0 and no opaque layer, and its slice of the elevated desk ignores background-opacity: check what it shows and match the grid's desk (T1852)

## Summary

Follow-up of T1852. Window.syncTranslucency lifts viewers and the rename box onto opaque layers (composeOpaqueLayer) but not the PaneSidebar child, whose expanded panel BitBlts a glass material built from the OPAQUE desk (deskBackdrop) and whose rows are GDI text. On a translucent window that is either see-through ink (GDI alpha 0) or an opaque slab beside a desk that now takes background-opacity. Measure with a WM_PRINTCLIENT/PrintWindow capture of the sidebar on a 0.5 window (the T1852 pane-elevation run 1b2 shape), then make its desk slice premultiplied at the same opacity (desk_palette.translucentPixel) with its rows and chrome opaque.

## In plain terms

With a see-through window (`background-opacity` below 1), the panes'
backdrop fades to let the desktop show through, but the sidebar column on
the left did not: its strip of backdrop stayed full strength, a solid slab
beside a faded grid. Now the sidebar's backdrop fades exactly as much as the
grid's, and the sidebar's card and its rows stay solid and readable.

## Goals

- [x] Measure what the sidebar shows on a 0.5-opacity window (PrintWindow capture, the T1852 shape): the rail's desk inset read 23,53,75, the same as the window toggled opaque, so it was an opaque slab.
- [x] Premultiply the sidebar's desk slice at the window's opacity, card and everything painted on it opaque.
- [x] The same picture in the live paint and in WM_PRINTCLIENT, so a capture shows what the screen does.

## Fix

`PaneSidebar.paintPremultiplied` (used by `paintBuffered` and
`WM_PRINTCLIENT` whenever `Window.translucent`): paints the frame into a
32bpp DIB, then premultiplies every pixel with
`desk_palette.translucentPainted(px, ref, desk, opacity)`. `ref` is the
card material cache (now keeps its `bits`, coverage in each alpha byte),
falling back to the bare desk mid-glide. A pixel the painter left matching
`ref` gets `translucentPixel`, the same function the grid's `PaneDesk`
uses, so the column's desk, its shadow and its card match the grid's
exactly. A pixel the painter touched (rows, text, the edge bar) is opaque.
The flat style uses the terminal background as the "desk", the way
`Window.paintAlphaCorrect` treats the chrome.
