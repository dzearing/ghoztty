# T2503 - Layered terminal panes (GHOZTTY_PANE_LAYER=1) fall behind a window-edge drag: resize-flicker.ps1 reads 12-15 stale frame waits of 11 vs 1-12 without the knob, 3-4 woken vs 5-7, and the panes' erase handler never runs on a window resize

## In plain terms

The fix for the graphics-memory leak (each pane drawing into its own surface) makes resizing the window by its edge measurably less smooth: more of the frames shown mid-drag are still drawn at the old size. Until that is fixed, the leak fix cannot be turned on for everyone.

## Summary

Measured 2026-10-30 by T2502 (debug build at db2584174, same box, alternating knob/plain runs of `test\win32\resize-flicker.ps1`, off-desktop):

| run | knob=1 stale / woken | plain stale / woken |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 15 / 3 | 8 / 7 |
| 2 | 13 / ? | 12 / ? |
| 3 | 14 / ? | 1 / ? |
| 4 | 13 / 3 | 11 / 5 |
| 5 | 12 / 4 | 3 / 7 |

(stale = drag frame waits that came back on a frame drawn at the OLD size, of 11 waits; woken = waits a presented frame actually woke). Means 13.4 vs 7.0. Plain HEAD is already red on the stale assertion (T2477), but the knob is consistently worse.

Second, deterministic difference (5 of 5 knob runs, 0 of 5 plain): `window resizes actually reached the erase handler (0 new decision(s))` fails - a WS_EX_LAYERED child pane gets no WM_ERASEBKGND on a top-level resize, so the `surface erase ...` decision log never fires and the "no pane blanks itself across window resizes" assertion is vacuous for layered panes.

On-screen look at 60 ms after a SetWindowPos resize showed no black or stale region (single sample, T2502 scratch photos), so the defect is frame-sync timing rather than a visible blank in a still - but it is the exact thing resize-flicker exists to hold.

Where to look: `src\apprt\win32\resize_paint.zig` (frame-drawn signal / wait), `Surface.zig` layered init (`Window.composeOpaqueLayer`), whether a layered child's present reaches the same `signalFrameDrawn` path, and whether the redirected layer surface needs the resize synchronised differently (DWM commits a layered child's new size on its own present).
