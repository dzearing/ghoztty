# T3676 - Linux L11a-4: signing out on Linux revokes THIS machine's relay enrollment, retries a revocation it could not finish, and restores it on the same account's sign-in

## Summary

The Linux half of win32 T1421/T1424 and Mac RelayAccount.signOut (docs/reference/remote-machines.md 'Signing out revokes THIS machine'): when the signed-in account owns this machine's relay.env enrollment, sign-out calls GET /v1/agent/whoami then POST /v1/agent/deenroll with the machine's own credential, deletes the local credential, and fails loudly (stays signed in) when the relay cannot be reached; Sign Out Anyway arms a pending revocation retried at launch and on network-back; the machine's name and relay are recorded before the POST so the same account's sign-in re-enrolls it. Needs the account row's sign-in/out (T3575). Split from T3563.

## In plain terms

Signing out on Linux is meant to take this computer off your account, so
your other computers stop listing it and can no longer open terminals on it.
When the relay could not be reached, the Linux chooser just said "couldn't
remove this machine" and left you stuck signed in, with no way forward. Now
it asks whether to sign out anyway; if you do, it says the machine is still
being removed, finishes the removal by itself once the relay answers (or the
next time the app starts), and signing back in with the same account puts
the machine back under its old name.

## Goals

- [x] a sign-out the relay cannot complete keeps you signed in, says why, and offers "Sign Out Anyway" / "Stay Signed In" (Enter stays)
- [x] "Sign Out Anyway" signs out, arms the revocation, and the signed-out row says the machine is still being removed
- [x] the armed revocation finishes by itself when the relay is back (while the app runs) and at the next launch
- [x] the machine's relay name is recorded before the de-enroll, so the same account's sign-in enrolls it again under that name
