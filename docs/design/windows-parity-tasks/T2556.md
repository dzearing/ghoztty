# T2556 - Composition step 5e: pointer-driven motion (divider, sidebar and drag-to-collapse) on the composition tree - choose WinRT property-set expressions vs per-input DirectComposition commits, measured, then build it

## Summary

T2360 asked for pointer-driven motion through a CompositionPropertySet expression. That is Windows.UI.Composition; the hosting chosen in T2466/T2508 is DirectComposition, which has no expressions. Decide (technical, ours): either a WinRT Compositor for the window tree (CreateDesktopWindowTarget, proven on a worker thread in the spike) so a divider/sidebar drag writes one property and expressions derive every card, or DComp with one offset write + commit per input message on a dedicated composition thread. Measure drag lag p95 and advance with the UI-stall seam both ways, then build the winner.

## In plain terms

When you drag the line between two panes, the panes should follow your mouse
as smoothly as they do on the Mac. This task was the choice of *how* to get
there on Windows. The choice is made: it is measured below, and building it is
two follow-up tasks.

## Goals

- [x] measure where a drag move's time goes today with pane-tree composition on
- [x] choose between WinRT property-set expressions and per-input DirectComposition writes
- [x] file the build as tasks with measurable validation (T2769, T2770)

## Decision (technical, ours - fails the experience test, so no D-file)

**DirectComposition, one offset/clip write + commit per input, ahead of a
coalesced resize pass (T2769); off the window thread on a compositor-clocked
cursor follower for stall resistance (T2770).** WinRT expressions are not
built.

Measured 2026-11-06, `test\win32\drag-perf.ps1` on HEAD 05e6b14fe with
`GHOZTTY_COMP_PANE_TREE=1 GHOZTTY_COMP_CHROME=1`, 2 panes, 25 ticks per drag:

| shape | mean per move | layout | of which pane batch (`place`) | cards | paint |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| serial | 15888 us | 15833 us | 14830 us | 1374 us | 987 us |
| fanout | 16529 us | 16463 us | 15472 us | 1042 us | 975 us |
| batched | 15693 us | 15629 us | 14661 us | 1054 us | 951 us |

Why:

- **About 93% of a move is the pane batch** (child-window moves and
  swap-chain resizes in `endPaneBatchFilled`), and about 1 ms is the
  card/compositor step. Expressions save only the per-card offset writes,
  which are already a small part of that ~1 ms. The lag is the pointer waiting
  on the resize, and either hosting fixes it the same way: move the visuals
  first and let the resize follow behind them.
- **Stall resistance does not depend on the hosting.** Both options need
  someone to write the pointer position each frame. Nothing from the system
  publishes mouse position to a win32 composition tree (the XAML pointer
  property set does not exist here, and `InteractionTracker` does not take
  mouse drags), so either way the write runs on a thread that reads the
  cursor. A DComp-device thread that waits on the compositor clock does that
  as well as a WinRT one would.
- **Cost of option A that buys nothing:** moving the window's whole tree
  (`chrome_comp.Host`, every overlay T2562 hung under the pane visuals, the
  glides T2572 declares) from DirectComposition to `Windows.UI.Composition`.
  That is a second hosting to maintain, and a re-validation of every
  composition step already landed.
- Mac parity check: a Mac divider drag runs on the main thread too, so T2769
  alone matches Mac. T2770 goes beyond it, and is kept because T2360's goal is
  motion that a busy window thread cannot stall.

The `drag-perf.ps1` FAIL rows in this run are T2690's stale wait-multiplication
assertions (guard acked to T2690). They are not a regression.
