# T2647 - divider-flicker.ps1 Lc scores any banner row that is not accent where the mid row is as a banner bite, so a torn bar (banner rows a frame behind the mid rows) and a ghost of the old bar in a grown terminal strip read as bites: classify each bitten frame by cause and fail only the banner-over-bar shape

## Summary

Found working T2642 (2026-11-02). `lib\DividerFlickerScore.ps1`'s BITTEN reading is "a banner row is not accent where the mid row is". Three different on-screen shapes satisfy it, and only the first is a banner bite:

1. **Banner over bar** (T2414/T2642): the strip stands over the bar, at its new rect ahead of the bar (grow) or its old rect behind it (shrink). Tell: in the banner rows the bar's columns read the banner strip's fill, and the bar's x on the mid row is the same as the frame before (grow) or the next frame's (shrink).
2. **Torn bar** (T2648): in the banner rows the bar is drawn whole, at its OLD x, while the mid rows show it at the new x. Tell: the banner rows have a full-width accent run, offset from the mid row's by one drag step. Frame 530 of `temp\divider-flicker\20261102-093351-none` (NoViewer): mid 1053, banner rows 1061.
3. **Ghost bar in a grown terminal strip**: the terminal pane that grew over the old bar shows the old bar for a frame in its new columns, BELOW the banner, while the banner and the band rows are right. Tell: the mid row has TWO accent runs, the old x and the new. Frame 107 of `20261102-092624-none`: mid 1021 (ghost), banner rows 1029 (the real bar), next frame 1029.

Fix: classify each bitten frame by those tells (the mid row's accent runs, the banner rows' accent run and its width, the neighbouring frames' bar x) and report each count apart; Lc fails on shape 1 only, and shapes 2 and 3 get their own reported lines (failing once T2648 and the ghost-bar task T2651 own them). Keep the scorer's synthetic sections (A-D) proving each shape is counted by its own reading and no other, the way B does for the existing readings.

## In plain terms

The automatic check that films a divider drag used to call three different
glitches the same thing, "a banner bit the pink line", so it could not tell
which one was left after a fix. Now it names each one: a banner covering the
line, the line's top part lagging a moment behind the rest, or a leftover
sliver of the old line. It fails only on the first and reports the other two,
each owned by its own task.

## What landed (2026-11-03)

- `DfScorer` (`test\win32\lib\DividerFlickerScore.ps1`) gets a per-frame
  `Kind`, computed only for a frame over `BiteBase`. **GHOST**: the mid line has
  two accent runs (each 2 px or wider) at least 2 px apart. **TORN**: EVERY
  banner line carries a bar-wide accent run (at least the mid width less 2 px)
  whose start is 2 px or more from the mid row's. **BITE**: anything else.
  GHOST is tested first. Runs under 2 px are dropped as anti-aliasing.
- `Get-DividerFlickerCounts` counts a bitten frame under exactly one of
  `BittenFrames` / `TornFrames` / `GhostFrames` (each with a Worst list). Lc
  still asserts `BittenFrames -eq 0`, so it now fails on the banner-over-bar
  shape only. The live cell prints `torn bar (reported until T2648)` and
  `ghost bar (reported until T2651)`.
- Neighbouring frames were NOT needed: every shape has a single-frame tell, so
  the scorer stays one pass and works on a frame stream.
- `docs\design\t2647-rescore.ps1` re-scores a saved live cell offline.
