# T1281 - Audit pixel assertions for oracles that cannot separate the outcomes they exist to separate

## In plain terms

Some of our automated colour checks are asking a question that cannot tell the
two answers apart - like judging whether a photo came out by measuring its
average brightness. They pass, so they are counted as checks, but they would
pass just as happily if the thing they are checking were broken. Two of these
were found in two days; this is the sweep for the rest.

## Summary

Two instances, both closed, both the same shape:

- **T363** (2026-09-02): chrome colour checks were scored against a literal
  rather than against the chrome, so they could not fail for the reason they
  existed.
- **T1115** (2026-09-02): `test-desktop-spike.ps1` asserted "PrintWindow returns
  real content" by scoring **mean luminance over the whole window** and
  requiring it to be bright. The terminal surface is dark by design and fills
  most of the window, so "light chrome over a flat, uncomposed client area"
  scores about the same as "the whole window captured" - the exact two outcomes
  the assertion existed to distinguish. It read green for a month while
  `lib\TestDesktop.ps1` had already measured and recorded the opposite answer,
  then flipped red with no behaviour change when the chrome shrank relative to
  the surface.

The generalisable defect: **an aggregate over a region that mixes the subject
with something else**. A whole-window mean, a whole-window distinct-colour
count, or any threshold satisfied by the chrome alone cannot speak about the
surface, and vice versa. The remedy that worked in T1115 was to score **by
region** and to keep a second region as the in-script positive control, so that
"the subject is flat" is a measurement rather than a broken capture.

## Where

- `test/win32/lib/TestDesktop.ps1` - `Get-TestWindowPixels` and the capture

## Dispositions

- **Live false-pass risks** (a mixed region on a claim the script reports as
  PASS) - filed, not fixed here, because each needs the geometry of its own
  window and an on-desktop run: T2208 `test-desktop-spike` CAP-1, T2209
  `title-font` lit count, T2210 `hero-mode` carousel hash, T2211
  `viewer-toc-emphasis` exact-wash match.
- **Lenient "real frame" gates** (~20, a whole-window distinct-colour floor
  ahead of regional asserts): SOUND as gates. Too easy a gate only lets the
  script reach its regional assertions, which then measure the unpainted
  surface and fail - it cannot turn a broken subject into a PASS.
- **Whole-capture helpers** in `lib/TestDesktop.ps1` / `lib/HoverCapture.ps1`:
  every current caller is a gate or passes a window that is the subject; the
  default itself is T2212.
- **What the static audit can and cannot see.** It reads two nested full-axis
  loops that accumulate - the T1115 shape, in PowerShell or embedded C#. It
  cannot see a whole capture handed to a helper as arguments (T1115's own fix
  routes through `SampleRect(0,0,w,h)` the same way), nor an aggregate taken by
  a library call; those are what T2212 narrows. The six walks it finds today
  (4 files) were each read and are SOUND - three are crops whose `$w/$h` are the
  crop's own size, two are `lib/SelectionBar.ps1`'s shape search for a solid
  10px saturated run, one is `pane-banner.ps1` counting link ink in an overlay
  that holds only the card - and are a per-file count in
  `test/win32/pixel-oracle-audit.baseline.json`.
