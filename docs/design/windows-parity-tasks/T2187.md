# T2187 - Unfolding a folded pane is instant on Windows, not the smooth slide Mac shows

## Summary

User report 2026-10-23: 'when i expand them the expand is instant and not smooth like on mac'. Expanding a folded strip (click it - T2109 - or drag the divider back - T2124) snaps to the final layout in one frame. T2102 claimed the other panes glide into place; on the user's installed release they do not, so either the slide is not wired on these paths, it is disabled by a config/DPI/release-build condition, or its duration rounds to zero. Verify against the user's build (win-v1.43.4/1.43.5), find which path skips the animation, and make every unfold slide with Mac's timing. Acceptance: frame-sampled capture of a strip click and of a drag-back shows intermediate widths across the slide, on a release-lineage build as well as Debug.

## In plain terms

When a pane had been folded down to a thin strip and you brought it back, it
just popped into place at the end instead of sliding in the way it does on
Mac. The slide was actually running - but the pane next to it stayed at its
old, wider size until the slide finished, and sat on top of the returning
pane the whole way. Now the neighbour narrows to its new size the moment the
unfold starts, so you can watch the returning pane glide in.

## Goals

- [x] find which path skips the visible slide (it was not skipped: it was covered)
- [x] a pane that shrinks during a slide shows only its new card from the first frame, without being resized mid-slide
- [x] the incoming pane is never under a neighbour on its way in, in both pane styles

## Root cause

Not a missing animation. T2102's slide runs on every unfold path (strip click,
drag back, grip pull) - `pane_stack_slide.plan` includes the returning card -
but it holds every pane that changes size at its OLD size until the slide ends
(no ConPTY resize mid-slide). An unfold GROWS the run, so the pane beside the
returning one shrinks, and held at its old width it lay over the returning
pane's entire path. Terminal children are `WS_CLIPSIBLINGS`, so the returning
pane was clipped away under it for all 380 ms and appeared in one frame when
the slide ended and the neighbour took its new size. The T2102 acceptance only
ever exercised equal-size swaps (a stacked row's run moving, never growing),
where no card is held bigger than its slot.

Mac (`PaneCardView.apply`) gives a resizing card its NEW frame at once and
freezes only the content inside it. The Windows translation: the pane window
stays held at its old size (still no resize mid-slide), and a window region
cuts it to its new card (`pane_stack_slide.cardAt` / `clip`,
`Window.slideClip`, `applyCardRegions` in both styles). The desk card
(elevated) follows the card too, not the held window. The slide's end lays
everything out at its slot and drops the cut.
