# T1878 - The Windows pane sidebar cannot scroll: a long list (many panes, or all windows) runs off the bottom, and Mac's project headers stay pinned while their windows scroll under them

## Summary

Found while building T1829. The Windows pane sidebar lays its rows out from
the top and never scrolls: a window with many panes, or the all-windows outline
across several windows, runs off the bottom of the panel (and of the rail's
card) with no way to reach the rest; the roster is also capped at 64 items.
Mac's list is a ScrollView, and in the all-windows scope its project headers
are STICKY - pinned at the top while their windows scroll under them, on a
backdrop so the rows passing beneath stay legible (b4a3216cc).

Wanted: wheel and scrollbar scrolling in the pinned panel, the open card and
the rail (the design system's overlay scrollbar), the selected row kept in view
as focus moves, sticky project headers in the outline, and hit-testing / drop
geometry / tooltips that follow the scroll offset.

## In plain terms

The list of panes down the side of a Windows window could not scroll: with
more panes than fit, the extra ones were simply cut off at the bottom with no
way to reach them. Now the list scrolls with the mouse wheel or a thin
scrollbar, jumps to whichever pane you switch to, and - when showing every
window - keeps a project's name pinned at the top while its windows scroll
underneath, the way the Mac sidebar does.

## Goals

- [x] wheel scrolling in the pinned panel, the open card and the rail
- [x] a thin overlay scrollbar thumb that can be dragged
- [x] the focused pane's row (and the empty drop slot during a drag) scrolls into view
- [x] sticky project headers in the all-windows outline, on the panel's own material
- [x] clicks, drop rows and tooltips follow the scroll; nothing out of view is a target
- [ ] the 64-pane roster cap - filed as T1914

## Design

A pure pass, `src/apprt/win32/pane_sidebar_scroll.zig`, takes the layout as
laid out for a column as tall as its content and turns it into the scrolled
one: it finds the viewport (below the header and search field; on the rail,
below the pin), clamps the offset, moves every scrolled rect up by it, and pins
the project head whose section is passing under the top (pushed out by the
next top-level head, as SwiftUI's pinned section headers are). It is applied in
`PaneSidebar.layoutFor`, the one funnel every painter, hit test, drop target
and tooltip goes through, so they cannot disagree about where a row is; and
`pane_sidebar.hitTest` refuses a point outside the viewport. Per surface
(column, open card) offsets; a new selection reveals once, so refreshes never
fight the user's own scrolling. The thumb takes a press on its own height ahead
of the resize band it overlaps. Thumb arithmetic is local rather than
`Scrollbar.thumbRect`, because that module pulls in the terminal and this one
runs in every test lane.

## Validation criteria

- [x] a list taller than the panel scrolls with the wheel, and the rows past the fold are reachable - `test\win32\pane-sidebar-scroll.ps1` sections 1-2 (offset 195/195, last row in view, every row moved by the offset); unit tests in the none lane
- [x] the rail scrolls the same way - same script, section 7 (15 tiles, 174/174, last tile in view)
- [x] the focused pane's row is scrolled into view when focus moves - section 4 (`pane sidebar reveal`, row in view); unit test for the least move and the pinned-header clearance
- [x] a project header sticks to the top while its windows scroll beneath it - section 6 (`sticky=0`, the head held at the viewport top while the window head moved by the whole offset); unit test for the push-out by the next section
- [x] clicks, drops and tooltips land on the scrolled rows (acceptance script) - clicks: sections 3 and 6 (a row past the fold restores; a click on the pinned head folds the project, not the row under it); tooltips: section 1 (`tips view=10` of 14 rows); drop rows: clipped to the viewport by `pane_sidebar_scroll.visible`, unit-tested, and the drop path itself re-run by `pane-sidebar-drag.ps1`. The open card's wheel shares the column's code path and is not separately driven on the box.
