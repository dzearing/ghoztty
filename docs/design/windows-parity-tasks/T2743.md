# T2743 - Composition step 4d-4: a stack strip's blade shadow (and a card gliding into its strip, cast as its blade shadow alone) moves as a compositor visual instead of being baked into the desk

## Summary

Fourth split of T2511: the blade half of the shadow work.

**Scoped 2026-11-07 to the picture, as T2740 was for card shadows and T2742
for card bodies; the wiring and the on-screen oracle moved to T2796.**

`src/apprt/win32/blade_layer.zig`: a strip's blade shadow as one
premultiplied black layer (`render`), the strip's size plus its reach at the
collapse on every side, measured from the strip's own corner. `Key` holds
everything the pixels depend on (size, axis, which way it leans, collapse,
style, corner radius, and the mask) and never the strip's position.

- **Kept off the bands in front.** On the desk each blade's shadow goes
  down just before its own band, so it lands on the bands behind it and
  never on one in front. `Key.inStack(strips, i, ...)` records every band
  after `i` that the layer reaches, relative to the strip, and `render`
  clears the layer under each by that band's own coverage
  (`pane_stack_strip.bandCoverage` at the new `bandRadius`, which
  `paintWith` now uses too, so the two shapes cannot drift).
- **A card gliding into its strip** is a strip entry with `band` false: it
  casts its layer at its collapse and masks nothing. The collapse is in the
  key, so a narrowing card is a new layer each step - the question T2796
  answers with a measurement before wiring that case.

## In plain terms

Panes folded into a stack show as thin strips, each casting a small shadow
on the strip behind it. That shadow is painted into the background picture
today. This step makes the shadow as a picture of its own, ready for the
system compositor to place, and proves it looks the same as the painted one.
Nothing changes on screen yet; the next step (T2796) switches it on.

## Goals

- [x] A strip's blade shadow drawn as its own see-through picture
- [x] Laid over a background with no blade shadows, it gives the same pixels as today's painted ones
- [x] It never darkens a strip in front of it, as the painted one never does
- [x] A card sliding into its strip gets its shadow at the right strength
- [x] The picture never depends on where the stack is
- [x] Switching it on and the on-screen proof filed as their own task (T2796)
