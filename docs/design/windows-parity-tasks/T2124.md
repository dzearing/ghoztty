# T2124 - Stacked panes: dragging a divider or stack grip past a pane's minimum folds the next pane into a strip, and dragging back unfolds them in reverse (the collapse gesture)

## Summary

Split from T2084 (Mac 2811a3043 chain collapse). Windows has NO collapse gesture yet: wire PaneStackState.restack/restackShares (and pane_stack_layout squeezedShares) into the divider drag in split_resize.zig so a drag past a pane's 300 DIP minimum minimizes the next pane and keeps chaining, and dragging back restores them in reverse order. Also hit-test and drag the stack grips (pane_stack_grid Result.stack_handles), which T2108 left unwired. Unit tests in the none lane over the share math; acceptance via posted mouse messages on the test desktop (enumerated pane rects before/after). Unblocks T2119.

## In plain terms

On Windows, pushing the line between two panes toward a pane that is already
as small as it may go simply stopped: nothing gave way. On Mac the squeezed
pane folds into a thin strip at the edge, the next one folds too if you keep
going, and dragging back unfolds them again. Windows now does the same, and
the little grips between stacked panes can be dragged too: pull the grip at
the edge of the stack outward and a folded pane comes back.

## Goals

- [x] a divider dragged 48 DIP past a pane's minimum folds the outermost pane on that side into a strip
- [x] going on folds the next one (the chain); coming back unfolds them last-folded-first
- [x] a fold the drag is released on stays folded
- [x] the grip between two stacked cards resizes them, and folds a card squeezed past its minimum
- [x] the grip at a narrowed run's edge, pulled outward, brings the nearest strip back
- [x] the caret never stays in a pane that just folded: it moves to the nearest pane on show

## Design

`src/apprt/win32/stack_drag.zig` is a pure port of Mac's `RowDrag` and its
arms (`splitDidResize`'s fold branch, `splitDidDragStack`, `squeezeRow`,
`splitDidRestack`'s focus rule): every step restores the row's snapshot from
the press and re-applies the whole fold, so a drag is reversible however far
it went. The window feeds it pixels (the grid lays out in pixels, so the
48 DIP distance and the 300/200 DIP minimums are scaled). A squeezed divider
drag puts every split ratio back to the drag's snapshot, as Mac restores
`dividerDragOrigin.tree`. Grips take the same grab band as dividers (wider
than the gutter, T94) rather than Mac's gutter-only target, for consistency
with the win32 dividers; the gutter-only resizer change is part of T2084's
other children (noted on T2125).

**One deliberate departure from Mac** (`RowDrag.press`): Mac measures the fold
point from the side's side-by-side minimum alone. When a side is already
under it at the press (a window too narrow for it, so it is laid out
stacked), the first pixel of movement folded that whole side and it could
not come back during the drag - drag-perf.ps1's 8-pane window showed it, a
72 px nudge folding seven panes. Here the thresholds also reach the press
(`leading = min(min, press)`, `trailing = max(dim - min, press)`), so a side
that fits at the press behaves exactly as Mac's, and an over-full one folds
only 48 DIP beyond where it was grabbed. Mac half filed as T2133 (seat mac).
drag-perf.ps1's drag step went from 6 to 3 px so its peak (36 px) stays a
resize, which is what it measures.
