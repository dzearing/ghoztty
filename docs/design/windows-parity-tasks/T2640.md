# T2640 - Composition step 5c-4: a card folding into or opening out of its strip glides on the compositor too - its per-frame fade limit and its strip face move off the window's thread - so a stalled UI thread no longer freezes the folding card while its neighbours glide on

## Summary

Follow-up of T2572. T2572 declares the stack glide to DirectComposition for cards that glide as cards; a strip card (folding into or opening out of its strip) stays on frames, because its fade is limited per frame (T2442, Slide.fade/limitFade) and its face is a StripFace popup drawn from the window's thread. Under a UI stall the neighbours now glide on while the folding card freezes. Also left on the UI tick: the desk paint, gutters and window regions (the tick still runs; only declared pane placement left it). Validate with comp-glide-hold-onscreen.ps1 -Arms stall scoring the folding card, and the T2485 chain-fold film.

## In plain terms

When a pane folds into the thin strip at the side of a stack (or opens back out of it), the app still draws every frame of that fold itself. Since T2572 the panes beside it keep gliding when the app is briefly busy, but the folding pane freezes, so for a moment one pane stands still while its neighbours move. This task makes the folding pane glide the same way they do.

## Split (2026-11-06)

Too big for one turn, so it was split into four slices that each fit one, the way T2554 was. What forced the split, from reading the code:

- **The folding card is not a pane visual at all while it folds.** A card carrying its frame (T2457) hides its live pane, and what is on screen is `StripFace`: a layered top-level popup that `paintStripFace` repaints on the CPU every frame (band, name, and the carried frame at `content_alpha`) and commits with `UpdateLayeredWindow`. A separate window cannot be handed to the window's composition tree as a path, so the face has to become a visual first (**T2777**). Only then can its path be declared (**T2778**).
- **The per-frame fade limit does not have to move to the compositor.** `limitFade` (T2442) caps how much of the fade one frame crosses because a slow window-thread frame (70 ms) used to cross all of it at once. On the compositor's clock every frame is a display frame (8-16 ms), where the cap never bites (the 60 fps test already proves that), so the declared path is the plain follower. T2778 records that.
- **Two more face shapes behave differently**: the unfolding card, whose frame arrives mid-glide, and a strip card whose live pane still shows under a blended face (**T2779**).
- **The desk paint, gutters and window regions** the summary also names are a separate layer (the desk visual), so they are their own slice (**T2780**).

T2777 also closes the T2561 blind spot in tree mode: once the face is part of the window's composition tree, a window capture contains it.
