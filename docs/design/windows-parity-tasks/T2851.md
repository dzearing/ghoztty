## Summary

T2825 declares a strip that stays a strip and moves (StripGlide, T2417) as a band-and-name face on the compositor (`Window.declareMovingStrip`), and the desk paints only the part of it covered at both ends of the glide (`StripFace.still`, `deskStripTab`). It is proven by log on the background desktop (pane-sidebar-glide.ps1 section 6 run with GHOZTTY_COMP_PRESENT/COMP_CHROME/COMP_PANE_TREE: `stack strip face declared clip_left=95->308 ...`, one declaration). Nobody has filmed it.

Add a `strip` arm to scripts/comp-glide-hold-onscreen.ps1: five panes beside the rail so the first is a blade at the grid's leading edge (the pane-sidebar-glide.ps1 section 6 shape), then a posted click on the sidebar toggle with GHOZTTY_DEBUG_GLIDE_STALL_MS set. Score the blade's leading edge frame by frame (its band colour is `pane_stack_strip.stripRgb` of the background; force a background whose strip colour no other chrome uses if the default collides with the sidebar), and assert its longest still stretch stays under a quarter of the stall. A second assertion reads `stack strip face declared` from the log. -NoDeclare (GHOZTTY_GLIDE_DECLARE=0) must go red. The film is a ghost window at the bottom of the z-order, the way the other arms run.

## In plain terms

A test that films the pane sidebar opening while the app is deliberately made busy, and checks that the thin folded pane at the edge of the grid keeps sliding instead of freezing. Built and run; the film found two real glitches the earlier log-only check could not see (a frozen copy left behind, and the folded pane disappearing briefly before it lands), now filed to be fixed.
