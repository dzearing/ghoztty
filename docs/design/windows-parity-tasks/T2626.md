# T2626 - Composition: a busy light's ring on the focused card sits under the compositor glow layer (the GDI arm paints it over the glow) - lift the lights into their own visual above the glow layers

## Summary

T2574 put the focus glow in its own compositor layer over a desk composed without it. The busy light's ring is still applied into the desk, so on the focused card the ring now sits UNDER the glow layer, while the GDI arm paints it over the glow. Where the layer's alpha is non-zero (the glow's 8pt reach round the card), the ring is tinted by up to the glow's strength (24% dark / 50% light). Fix: the lights as their own visual above the glow layers (also takes their per-frame ring uploads off the desk surface). Oracle: the on-screen grab of a busy focused card matches the GDI arm round the ring.

## In plain terms

When a pane is busy, a light circles its border. On the newer drawing path
(off by default), the soft glow round the focused pane was drawn on top of
that light, so the light looked washed out on the focused pane. Now the
light is drawn above the glow, the way the normal path always drew it.

## Goals

- [x] the busy lights are their own compositor layer, over the focus glow's layers and under the overlay
- [x] their per-frame ring updates go to that layer, not to the desk surface
- [x] the layer laid over the desk is the picture the window draws with the rings in it (unit-tested)
- [x] the GDI path, a translucent window and a host whose layer failed keep the rings in the desk

## Design (2026-11-27)

`busy_light.apply`'s three blends toward one colour are one blend by
1 - (1-a1)(1-a2)(1-a3), so a ring is a premultiplied layer that does not
depend on what lies under it. `busy_light.applyLayer` writes that layer;
`PaneDesk.setLightsApart` (set by `Window.syncLightsApart` while the glow is
on the compositor and the window is opaque) stops drawing rings into `comp`
and sends the layer through a new `Sink.lights` hook - whole at a new size,
then the strips the last frame and this one laid. `chrome_comp.Host`
carries it as `lights_vis`, inserted under the overlay; glow layers now
insert under it, so the order is desk < glows < lights < overlay whichever
comes up first. Translucent windows stay as they were (their desk is `out`,
re-premultiplied, and the glow layers' unblend assumes an opaque picture).
