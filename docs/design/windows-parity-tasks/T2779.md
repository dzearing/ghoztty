# T2779 - Composition step 5c-4c: a card opening out of its strip glides on the compositor - its face declared band-first and again when its new-size frame lands, the fade rising in from a hold at 0 (the live-face half is T2822)

## Summary

Split of T2640, after 5c-4b (T2778). **Scope narrowed on claim (2026-11-08):** this task is the UNFOLD half; the live-face half went to **T2822**, and T2821 (filed by T2778's close for the same unfold half) was closed as its duplicate.

An UNFOLD (T2458): the face is the whole card, from the strip's band alone until the frame the pane draws at its new size lands (askOpeningFrame), then carrying that frame fading in. It was stepped from the window's thread, so a busy moment froze an opening card while its neighbours glided on. Now, in the composition pane tree, it is declared to DirectComposition the way T2778 declares a fold:

- `Window.declareStripFace` takes `carried: ?Content` and `opening`: band + name with an empty content picture until the frame lands, then a new declaration from where the compositor has the face (`FaceDecl.reached`) carrying the frame. `FaceDecl.content` (a `ContentId` of the frame) makes a landed frame - or one asked again at another size by a drag - a new declaration, like a moved landing.
- The fade runs the other way: an unfold's strength line starts below 0 and rises in part-way. `strip_face.fadeLine` keeps both ends unclamped (null only above 1), `comp_tree.FacePose.fadeSpan` gives the stretch of the way the line is inside 0..1 (replacing T2778's end-only `fadeKnee`), `FaceDecl.fadeTimes` turns it into enter/leave seconds, and `chrome_comp.Host.followAnimationHeld` holds the value flat before the enter (its piece re-expanded there with `comp_overlay.tailOf`) and after the leave.
- Oracle knob `GHOZTTY_DEBUG_GLIDE_STALL_AT=unfold` (debug builds): the `GHOZTTY_DEBUG_GLIDE_STALL_MS` stall lands once the opening card's content is well in (content_alpha >= 153), so the film has seen it before the window's thread blocks.

## In plain terms

When you click a folded pane's strip to open it, the pane slides out of its strip and its content fades in. If the app was busy for a moment while that happened, the opening pane froze mid-way and then jumped, while the panes around it kept sliding. Now Windows itself runs the whole opening, so it keeps moving smoothly even when the app is busy. (Only in the new composition rendering path.)
