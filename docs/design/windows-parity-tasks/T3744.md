# T3744 - Linux: closing a pane beside a collapsed row's strip focuses the strip and brings it back out on GTK - the close's focus target must be the pane on show (PaneStackState.paneOnShow), as win32 and Mac do

## Summary

Found during T3627. GTK's closeConfirmationClose (src/apprt/gtk/class/split_tree.zig) picks next_focus as the tree's previous/next leaf (goto), with no regard for stacking: when that neighbour is minimized in a collapsed row, the rebuild focuses it and propSurfaceFocused relays the stage, so the strip comes back out and the user's collapse is undone by an unrelated close. win32 routes the close's caret through PaneStackState.paneOnShow(candidate, old_rows, new_rows) (Window.paneOnShowAfter) and Mac does the same. Port it: compute rows before/after the removal (SplitTree.stackRowsNow, added by T3627) and take paneOnShow's answer. Acceptance: a test/linux/stack-persist.sh section - three panes, the user collapses the leading one, close the focused middle neighbour: the caret goes to the pane on show and the strip stays a strip.

## In plain terms

On Linux, when you squeeze a pane down into a thin strip at the edge of a row
and then close the pane next to it, the keyboard focus could be handed to that
strip, which makes it pop back open and undoes what you did. Now the close
sends focus to the pane that is actually showing, and the strip stays a strip,
the same as on Mac and Windows.

## Goals

- [x] A pane close on GTK sends the caret through `PaneStackState.paneOnShow` (rows before vs after the removal), as win32's `paneOnShowAfter` does
- [x] An acceptance section in `test/linux/stack-persist.sh` covers three panes, the leading one tucked, the focused middle one closed

## Validation criteria

- [x] P16-P20 of `test/linux/stack-persist.sh` (wsl-run, clone of 034c947430): qa | qb | qc in order, qa tucked into a strip, qb closed - the caret lands in qc and qa is still a strip a second later. ALL PASS (P1-P21).
- [x] P21: the app logs `closed pane's focus goes to the pane on show, not a strip`, so the close's own target was the strip and `paneOnShow` redirected it (it is not left to GTK declining to focus a strip).
- [ ] NOT CONFIRMED: the pre-fix symptom. On a build without the fix (a run with the panes in the wrong order) the caret still reached the pane on show; a correctly-ordered red run was not taken (the `-Repo mnt` build fails on a 9P cache rename). It is likely that GTK quietly refuses focus on a minimized strip and the caret falls through, so the user may never have seen the strip pop open. The fix makes the close's target explicit either way.
