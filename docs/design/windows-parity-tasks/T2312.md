# T2312 - On Windows a strip already on a stack shifts while another pane collapses onto it; Mac's stays put - it moves by its outer edge and takes its 12pt reach under the newcomer only once the glide is over (Mac 760272563)

## Summary

Mac 760272563 (ref: main docs/reference/stacked-panes.md): when another pane collapses between a strip and the run, the strip nearest the run gains the 12pt blade tuck (bladeTuck) under the newcomer. Mac used to apply that reach during the glide, so a leading strip's edge poked into the gutter ahead of the newcomer and a trailing strip's outer edge jumped out and eased back. Now a strip that stays a strip moves by its OUTER edge (the one that shows) during the glide and takes the extra reach only when the glide is over, once the newcomer has landed over it (PaneStageView.stepGlide; test aStripStaysPutWhenAnotherCollapsesOntoIt). Windows blades landed in T2283 (a50ce35ae); check its glide for the same jiggle and port the ordering.

## In plain terms

When a second pane folds into a stack that already has a folded pane on it, the existing bar briefly stuck out past where it was, into the gap ahead of the pane still sliding in. Now it stays exactly where it was until the newcomer has landed on top of it, as on Mac.

## Goals

- [x] A strip that stays a strip keeps its length, by its outer edge, while the glide runs
- [x] It takes its 12 DIP reach under the newcomer once the glide is over; losing reach stays immediate
- [x] The flat style's background fill and the strip's name follow the held length

## What Windows did (re-verified 2026-10-27)

Not Mac's shape exactly: a strip that stays a strip is not in the Windows glide at all (`pane_stack_slide.plan` skips tucked -> tucked), so it was painted at its NEW layout tab from the first frame. Its outer edge never moved (no trailing jump), but its 12 DIP reach under the newcomer appeared at once - in the gutter ahead of the card still narrowing into its strip, the leading-side half of Mac's defect, on both sides here.

## Fix

`plan` records each strip that stays a strip and whose tab grew along the stack (`holdReach`: its old length, or less if an interrupted glide was holding it shorter). `Slide.shownStrip` cuts the laid-out tab to that length by its outer edge while the glide exists; `Window.shownStripTab` is the one place the painters read it - `tuckedStrips` (the desk's bands and blade shadows, the strip names, with the name's tuck reduced by what is not reached yet) and the flat style's background clip. The glide ending (`endStackSlide`) drops the hold and the next layout paints the full reach.
