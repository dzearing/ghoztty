# T3629 - Linux L11e-6: strips on GTK relabel live and are drag sources - +set-banner / OSC title changes rename a showing strip at once, and dragging a strip picks its pane up (PaneDropResolver.resolveBlade targets)

## Summary

Follow-up of T3567 (docs/reference/stacked-panes.md 'Strip labels' live rule, 'Dragging a strip'). The GTK strip face takes its label (pane_sidebar_rows.text, the sidebar's rule) only when the stage is laid out, so a title or banner change while a pane is minimized shows on the strip only at the next layout; and a strip is not a drag source. Relayout (or relabel) the stage on the surface's title/banner notify, and give each strip face a PaneDrag source with the blade drop targets. Also verify a COLUMN (vertical row) stacks with horizontal strips - stacked-panes.sh only drives rows. Acceptance: stacked-panes.sh sections for a live relabel via +set-banner, a column stack, and a strip drag through the pane-drag seam.

## In plain terms

On Linux, when a row of panes is too narrow and some panes fold into thin
strips, a strip used to keep its old name until something else made the row
lay out again, and you could not drag a strip anywhere. Now a strip is renamed
the moment its pane's banner or title changes, dragging a strip moves its pane
along the row (or to the window's edge) the way it does on the Mac, and a
column of panes stacks with strips lying across it.

## Goals

- [x] A strip relabels at once on `+set-banner`, a cleared banner, and an OSC 2 retitle
- [x] A strip is a drag source whose drop has a strip's own targets (row slot, window edge; nothing over a card)
- [x] A strip dropped among the cards on show is focused; one dropped among strips stays one
- [x] A minimized pane is a drop target for an ordinary pane drag by its strip, not its hidden card
- [x] A column stacks, with horizontal strips and vertical gutters

## Design notes

- **Relabel.** `SplitTree.relabelStrip` renames a showing face at once
  (`labelFace`, the sidebar rule `pane_sidebar_rows.text`) and re-lays the
  stage out; it runs on the surface's `title`/`title-override`/`pwd` notify
  and from `Surface.setPaneBanner` (+set-banner and OSC 7778 both land there).
- **Strip drag.** Every face carries a `PaneDrag.StripSource` (a
  `GtkDragSource`, keyed by the stage's pane key so a face never holds a
  surface pointer). `stageDragBegin` leaves a strip press UNCLAIMED, so the
  drag source claims it past GTK's drag threshold and a shorter release stays
  the stage gesture's click (focus). A strip released over nothing settles
  back (no pop-out window, unlike a header drag).
- **Drop.** `dropPaneAt(.., blade)` resolves with `pane_drop.resolveBlade`
  over `bladeGeometry` (win32's, from the grid's placements), and
  `pane_drop_apply.apply` now makes `.reorder` (pure, unit-tested). Focus
  follows win32 `commitReorderDrop`: lay the new tree out, focus the pane only
  if it is not minimized there.
- **No screen-edge tile on Linux.** Mac/win32 tile a strip dropped at a
  screen's edge into a half-screen window. Wayland tells a client neither its
  monitors' layout relative to its window nor where its window is, so GTK
  passes no screens and that target does not exist; the release settles back,
  which is what Mac does anywhere else. Recorded as a platform limit, not a
  gap to file: there is no Wayland mechanism to build it on.
