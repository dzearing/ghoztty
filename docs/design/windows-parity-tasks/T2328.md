# T2328 - No on-box acceptance proves a Windows strip holds still while another pane collapses onto the stack (T2312 landed with unit tests only; the stacked-pane harnesses cannot form a stack until T2295)

## Summary

T2312 (Mac 760272563) holds a strip that stays a strip at its old length, by its outer edge, while another pane collapses between it and the run, and gives it its 12 DIP reach (`blade_tuck`) only once the glide is over (`pane_stack_slide.Slide.shownStrip`, `Window.shownStripTab`). It is proved by a unit test in `pane_stack_slide.zig` only: nothing on the box watches the strip during a real collapse.

Mac's proof is `aStripStaysPutWhenAnotherCollapsesOntoIt` (both sides): tuck one pane, drag the next divider to collapse a second onto it, sample the first strip every 4 ms during the glide, and assert its outer edge never moves and no more of it shows than before.

Port it as a section of `test\win32\pane-stack-strip.ps1` (or a sibling) with `GHOZTTY_STACK_SLIDE_MS` stretching the glide, reading the strip band's pixels from the desk at a few points mid-glide on each side: the band must not appear in the gutter between the old strip and the arriving card. Needs T2295 first - the stacked-pane harnesses no longer form a stack at the 225 DIP minimum.

## In plain terms

When you squeeze a pane until it folds away onto a stack of folded panes, the
folded pane already sitting there should stay perfectly still while the new one
slides in. That was fixed earlier, but only a code-level test checked it. Now a
test drives the real app - drags the dividers, watches every frame of the slide
- and fails if the folded pane ever creeps into the gap ahead of the newcomer.

## Goals

- [x] An on-box harness that folds one pane, then folds the next onto it, on both sides of the row
- [x] It watches the first strip on every frame of the glide and checks its edge and length
- [x] A negative control that turns the hold off and must go red

## Decision (mechanism, not filed)

The card suggested reading the strip band's pixels from the desk. The harness
instead reads a debug-build oracle line, `stack strip shown`
(`Window.logShownStrips`), logged on every glide tick with the rect the strip is
drawn at (`shownStripTab`) and the rect the layout has it at. That is the exact
translation of Mac's proof, which samples the model's `placedTab` every 4 ms
rather than pixels, and it sees every frame instead of a few pixel probes. The
negative control is a debug-only knob, `GHOZTTY_TEST_NO_STRIP_HOLD=1`, that
drops the plan's held strips (the answer before T2312); it compiles out of
release builds.

## Validation criteria

- [x] Leading and trailing: the held strip's outer edge stays put and none of its band shows in the gutter ahead of the arriving card, sampled mid-glide on the test desktop - `test\win32\pane-stack-strip-hold.ps1` H1: 182 (trailing) / 186 (leading) glide ticks with the layout's reach already given, 0 moved, 0 longer than the 45 px before (120 DPI)
- [x] Once the glide settles the strip reaches 12 DIP under the newcomer (the T2283 layout) - H2: landed 45 -> 60 px (+15 px = 12 DIP at 120 DPI), same outer edge, both sides
- [x] `-NegativeControl` (no hold) fails the mid-glide check - observed 2026-11-26: H1 "no more of it shows" FAILS on both sides (195/196 and 186/187 ticks longer; the strip creeps 45 -> 60 px through the glide)
