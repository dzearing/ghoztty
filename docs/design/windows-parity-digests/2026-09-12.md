## Commentary

### Yesterday

**The loop was not broken — it had run out of money.** From Tuesday evening
until 1am this morning, nothing happened at all: 63 hours of silence, no
crash, no error on screen. The account had hit its monthly spend limit, so
every attempt to restart was refused the instant it was made, and the
supervisor kept trying the same rescue 180 times, reporting the outage 716 times as nothing but "no
turn has completed", which is a symptom rather than a reason. The limit reset
at 01:00 and the loop came straight back on its own.

**So the first four things it did were about never losing a weekend that way
again.** The status readouts now say the real thing ("blocked: you've hit your
monthly spend limit, clears 2026-09-12 01:00") instead of a bare stall, and the
dashboard shows it on screen rather than waiting for somebody to type a command
(**T1483**, **T1484**). Telling the loop to stop and then start again now
actually starts it, and refuses to claim success unless the loop is genuinely
running (**T1478**). And the build machine was failing every Windows build
while installing an unrelated packaging tool, so the one signal that says "the
branch compiles" had been saying nothing — that is fixed (**T1481**).

**Two things you can see landed too.** Dragging the bar between two panes no
longer flashes an empty terminal for a frame — the window now waits for a
correctly-sized picture before letting the resize land (**T1476**). And a tab's
top-edge highlight is now exactly the banner card's, instead of a near-miss
that made tabs read slightly shinier than the card below them (**T679**).

**What shipped:** `win-v1.36.21`, cut at 01:14 this morning as soon as the loop
woke up. **What has not:** twelve commits have landed since — the whole
spend-limit visibility set, the divider-flash fix, the tab highlight, and the CI
repair. None of that is on your machine yet; tonight's publish carries it.

### Reflection

**The recurring theme is now unmistakable: our instruments report liveness, not
work.** Three separate outages this month had the same shape — a pid was alive,
a transcript was moving, a check was green, and nothing was being accomplished.
Each one was closed by teaching one readout to measure the real thing. The next
step is to stop doing that one readout at a time. There is a general rule
waiting to be written: *every health field names the thing it cannot see*, so a
new one cannot ship with a blind spot nobody wrote down.

**And the second theme: reasons live in the transcript, and nothing reads it.**
The answer to this outage was sitting in plain English in the loop's own pane
for 63 hours while four different status commands said "stalled". `-Postmortem`
exists precisely to print that tail, and the fix that landed today was, in
essence, "read the pane before theorising". That generalises further than the
spend limit — most of our mysterious stalls have had their explanation on
screen the whole time.

**What still stands between here and the cutover to `main`:** unchanged, and
not this seat's — the Mac regression build (**T87**) and the `origin/main`
commits that must be merged into this branch first. `ship-readiness.ps1` names
both on every run.

## Feature ideas

### For the person using the terminal

- **Explain what just went wrong, without leaving the terminal** (High) —
  select a stack trace or a failed command's output, right-click, "explain
  this", and the answer opens in a viewer pane beside it. The thing you
  actually do next after a red build, done in place.
- **Every keybinding, one keypress away** (High) — a searchable overlay of the
  live config's bindings. Nobody remembers the chords, and today the only way
  to find one is to open a config file.
- **Change a setting from the command palette** (High) — search settings by
  name and toggle them where you already are, instead of editing a file and
  reloading. The palette already knows how to find things; settings are the
  obvious next thing for it to find.
- **Windows come back where you left them, per monitor** (Medium) — remember
  size and position keyed to the monitor layout, so undocking a laptop and
  redocking it does not pile every window onto the built-in screen.
- **Type the split you want** (Medium) — set a divider to an exact ratio or
  column count ("60/40", "80 columns") instead of dragging until it looks
  right. Half the drags people make are aiming at a number they already know.
- **Protect a pane from an accidental close** (Medium) — pin a pane so Ctrl+W
  and the close button ask first. The long-running build is always the one that
  gets closed.
- **Tell me the instant a remote pane drops, and offer the way back** (Medium) —
  a toast plus a one-click reconnect on the pane itself, rather than finding out
  by typing into a dead session.

### Underneath

- **A crash that files its own report** (Medium) — when the app dies, the next
  launch opens a card with the captured stack already attached and one button to
  send it. The tooling to capture it exists; what is missing is the hand-off to
  the person who saw it happen.
