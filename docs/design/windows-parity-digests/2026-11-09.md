nothing recovers it.** Session persistence did its job yesterday: both loop
lanes' sessions kept running with no window. But the windows stayed gone
until something noticed. On Mac a crash reporter at least tells you. Here,
relaunching and re-attaching should take a second, not a person.

and reverted), and it found the real cost: one system call that is not ours

## Feature ideas

### What you see and press

- **Back in a second after a crash** - *High.* If Ghoztty ever goes down,
  it relaunches by itself and puts every window back with its live sessions
  attached. A small note says it happened. You lose a second, not your
  layout. (New angle on crash restore: no prompt, nothing to click, because
  the sessions never died.)
- **The mouse's side buttons step back** - *Medium.* The back and forward
  buttons on your mouse step between earlier commands in a terminal pane and
  between pages in a viewer pane, the way they do in a browser.
- **Status you can read without colour** - *Medium.* Busy, done and failed
  look different by shape as well as colour (a spinner, a tick, a cross).
  If you cannot tell red from green, the sidebar still tells you which pane
  needs you.
- **Scroll two panes together** - *Medium.* Link two panes and they scroll
  as one, so you can compare two logs or two runs line by line. (New angle on
  comparing panes: the panes stay live, with no diff view.)

### Stability and performance

- **A graphics driver fault costs one window, not all of them** - *High.*
  When the graphics driver fails while drawing one window, that window
  switches to safe drawing and carries on. Your other windows never notice.
