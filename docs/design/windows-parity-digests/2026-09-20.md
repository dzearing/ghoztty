Yesterday's shape was **user-facing work coming out of floor work**, repeatedly.
T891 landed the palette and immediately produced T1671 (clicking the wrong row
once the list scrolls) and T1676 (offering an update that does not exist). T1673

actually used — but it is also the arithmetic of a backlog that will not
converge: 18 closed, 18 filed, net zero, for the second day running.

The thing worth changing is not the filing. It is that **most of what gets filed
from a fix is the same fix's unfinished edge**, and we let it become a new card
at the same priority as an unrelated one. A follow-up that is one commit of
polish on work you have loaded in your head today is worth strictly more
finished today than queued for a turn three weeks from now that will pay the
context cost again. Worth trying: when a card produces follow-ups that are
*inside its own surface*, finish the cheap ones in the same turn and file only
what genuinely needs its own investigation.

The second observation is about the P1 band being empty. For weeks the queue has
been answering "what is the most valuable thing" with a P2, because everything
genuinely important is dep-blocked on the cutover. **The dependency graph is
telling us the project's next move and we keep reading it as a scheduling
detail.** `ship-readiness.ps1` names the same two gaps every time it runs; one of
them (T87) belongs to a seat that is not turning. That is the thing to bring to
you, not another harness card.

## Feature ideas

### Making the palette worth living in

- **Type what you want, not what it is called** — *High.* The palette should
  match on what a command does as well as its name, so "make this bigger" finds
  the font-size commands and "get rid of this pane" finds close. Today you have
  to already know the vocabulary the app chose.
- **Run the last thing again, from anywhere** — *Medium.* One entry at the top
  of the palette that repeats your last action, whatever pane you are in. The
  palette already knows what you use; it does not yet know what you just did.

### Knowing what you are about to install

- **See the update's changes in the balloon itself** — *High.* The notification
  names a version; what you want to know is which of the things that annoy you
  got fixed. Put two or three plain-language headlines on the card, with a link
  to the rest.
- **Hold this update until I am done** — *Medium.* A "not while I am working"
  choice that stops the badge nagging and applies the update the next time you
  close the window, instead of making you choose between now and forgetting.

### Shells that configure themselves

- **Tell me my shell is not fully wired up** — *High.* When integration cannot
  attach — an unusual path, a flavour we do not recognise, a profile that
  overrides it — the pane should say so once, in a line you can dismiss, rather
  than silently losing command marks and directory tracking.
- **Show me the shell this pane actually started** — *Medium.* Not the one the
  config asked for: the executable, the flavour, and whether integration is
  live, on hover over the tab. Two of yesterday's fixes exist because the answer
  to "what am I actually running" was invisible.

### When something is wrong with the app itself

- **A health card you can open** — *Medium.* One panel that answers the
  questions that today need a script: which build is this, how far behind the
  release is it, is the session agent up, is anything on the share stale. The
  loop has all of this; the person sitting at the terminal has none of it.
