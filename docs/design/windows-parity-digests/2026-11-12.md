### Reflection

**A reboot cost more than a day of work, and it can be prevented.**
Windows Update restarted the PC at 00:31, and without a sign-in nothing of
ours can start. The loop's watchdog, its scheduled task and its Run entry
all need a signed-in desktop. The fix is one switch only you can turn on:
*Settings > Accounts > Sign-in options > "Use my sign-in info to
automatically finish setting up my device after an update or restart"*.
This is the third reboot outage the claim has reported. It is now the
single largest source of lost loop time.

**A turn killed by the reboot lost nothing, because the tracker recorded
its progress.** T1480 was validated and logged when the reboot hit, but not
committed. Today's turn found it uncommitted in the tree, checked the
diff against the task's progress log and committed it. Writing to the
progress log before the commit is what saved it.

**The cost per smoothness fix is falling.** Yesterday each fold and drag
fix was measured in dropped frames per run and shipped the same day. What
still stands between those numbers and "smooth" is a film on your real
screen. Every remaining P0 waits on that, not on code.

## Feature ideas

### What you see and press

- **Double-click picks the whole thing** - *High.* Double-click a file
  path, web address, GUID or IP address and the whole of it is selected.
  Today a double-click stops at the first slash or dash, and you drag
  instead.
- **A folder that is gone never holds up a new pane** - *High.* Open a
  split while the current folder is on a network drive that has dropped.
  The pane opens at once in your home folder, with one line naming the
  folder it could not reach. It does not sit blank waiting for the network.
- **Keep my splits when Windows snaps the window** - *Medium.* Snap a
  window to half the screen and back, and every divider returns to exactly
  where you left it. Today the panes can come back squeezed to new
  proportions.
- **Mark where the PC slept** - *Medium.* A faint line in the scrollback
  says "PC asleep 2h 10m" at the point the machine slept. Timings and
  timestamps around it then make sense.
- **A pane waiting on you reaches your phone** - *Medium.* When a pane
  starts waiting on an answer and the PC is locked, Windows Phone Link
  shows it on your phone with the question.

### Under the hood

- **Paste into a slow remote never drops a character** - *Medium.* A long
  paste into an SSH or serial session is fed at the pace the far end
  accepts, so nothing is lost on a slow link. (New angle on the huge-paste
  idea: the limit is the far end's speed, not ours.)
