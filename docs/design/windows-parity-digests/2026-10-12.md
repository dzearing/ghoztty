seven weeks and took a day-long merge to close. This time the gap lasted one

## Feature ideas

### What you see and press

- **"Pane closed - Undo", right where you closed it** — *High.* Closing a pane
  shows a small note at the bottom of the window for a few seconds with an Undo
  button. People find out undo exists at the one moment they need it, without
  learning a shortcut first.
- **Peek at a parked pane without bringing it back** — *High.* Hover a stashed
  pane's row in the sidebar and a live miniature of it appears beside the row.
  You can check whether the build finished, or what Claude asked, and leave it
  parked.
- **Busy panes lift off the desk** — *Medium.* With the new raised-card look, a
  pane that is running something sits a little higher with a deeper shadow. A
  pane waiting on you lifts higher still. You read the state of the whole window
  from across the room, with no badges.
- **Recently closed, at the foot of the sidebar** — *Medium.* *New angle on
  bringing back a closed pane:* the panes you closed in the last minute stay at
  the bottom of the sidebar, greyed out. Click one to put it back. Undo becomes
  something you can see and pick from, not a stack you press through blind.
- **Cards shaped like Windows 11 windows** — *Medium.* The rounded panes use the
  same corner radius and shadow as Windows 11's own windows and follow the
  system when that changes. The new look reads as part of Windows rather than a
  Mac look carried across.

### Stability and performance

- **An undo that outlives a crash** — *Medium.* A pane you closed seconds before
  the app crashed or restarted can still be undone afterwards, because its session
  is kept for the length of the undo window. The worst moment to lose work stops
  being worse.
